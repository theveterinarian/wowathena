Can you take DUI school online in Georgia?

Self-paced online DUI courses don't count in Georgia. Live virtual classes taught in real time by a DDS-certified school do. Here's how to tell them apart and what a live online class asks of you.

By Athena DUI Academy & Family EnrichmentLast reviewed September 28, 20267 min read

Key takeaways

  • The Georgia DDS does not accept certificates from online DUI courses that you click through on your own time.
  • Georgia rules do allow live remote classes: a DDS-certified program may teach DUI school over video if the instruction is synchronous, meaning real time, at a set time, with a live instructor.
  • The same rule lets certified programs give the assessment remotely, following DDS guidelines. Ask your school how it handles it.
  • In a live virtual class you must stay on camera for every session, log in on time, stay seated and away from distractions, and never attend from a moving vehicle.
  • Live virtual DUI school costs the same $360 as in-person classes and leads to the same Certificate of Completion.

You can't take Georgia DUI school as a self-paced online course, because the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) does not accept certificates from online DUI courses. You can take it live online: Georgia's rules let DDS-certified programs teach the class over video, in real time, with a certified instructor, and that class counts the same as one in a classroom.

The official name for DUI school is the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program. The words "online" and "virtual" get used loosely, so the rest of this page explains exactly which kind of class DDS accepts and what a live virtual class expects from you.

Online DUI school vs. live virtual DUI school

The difference comes down to one thing: whether a certified instructor is teaching you live, at a set time. DDS says certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses "cannot be accepted by the DDS for any purposes related to an individual's driver's license or driving privileges." That covers the kind of course you click through alone, whenever you like.

What the Georgia rules say about remote DUI classes

The rules for Risk Reduction Programs are in Chapter 375-5-6 of the Georgia rules and regulations. Rule 375-5-6-.18 says a certified program "may utilize remote services for classroom instruction and the administration of assessments," through web services "such as, but not limited to, Skype, Zoom, or Webex."

The same rule sets the condition that makes it different from an online course: "Instruction must be synchronous allowing for real-time instruction and interaction in a specific virtual place, through a specific online platform, at a specific time." Programs that teach remotely must also notify DDS at least 24 hours before class starts and stay subject to all DDS monitoring and auditing.

Rule 375-5-6-.02 defines a "Remote Classroom" as "a certified program's online classroom where synchronous instruction is provided by an Instructor." DDS's own remote classroom guidelines put it plainly: remote instruction happens at a scheduled time with the instructor and students, and "this is not online instruction that is completed at the student's discretion."

The short version: if a real DDS-certified instructor is teaching you live, at a scheduled time, on a video platform, it can count. If you're watching videos alone and clicking "next," it won't.

Can you do the DUI assessment remotely?

Yes, Georgia's rules allow it. DUI school has two parts: a 130-question assessment that screens your alcohol and drug use, and the 20-hour class. Rule 375-5-6-.18 covers both when it allows remote services for "classroom instruction and the administration of assessments."

The DDS policies guide adds that assessments may only be given under the DDS remote classroom guidelines or at the program's certified location, and in a private setting free from distractions. How a school runs the assessment is up to that school, so ask when you register whether you'll take it remotely or at the office.

What a live virtual DUI class expects from you

DDS holds remote students "to the same standards as if they were in an actual classroom setting." Its remote classroom guidelines list what that means. They say the list isn't complete, so your school may have more rules, but these are the ones DDS spells out:

  • Stay on camera. You must be visible for every session, apart from breaks. Audio only, a still photo or a view of the ceiling doesn't count.
  • Log in on time and stay to the end. You can't join late or leave early.
  • Sit somewhere quiet. You need to be seated and away from distractions. No lying in bed, working, shopping, cooking, babysitting or talking to others.
  • Never attend from the road. You can't drive while you're in class.
  • Only you. Only students who contracted for the course can attend, so no children or roommates in the class, apart from accommodations for students who need help to read or write or who have a disability.
  • Keep your background appropriate. Nothing showing alcohol, illegal drugs or anything offensive.
  • No recording, drinking, vaping or tobacco during sessions.
  • Check your tech first. Download the platform before class and make sure your internet, power and data will last the whole session.

Take these seriously. DDS says students who arrive late to any session can expect to be denied entry, and students who are absent or leave early may need to start the course over and pay the full cost again. A dropped connection or a camera turned off can cause the same problems as walking out of a classroom.

How to spot an online DUI course Georgia won't accept

DDS says classes taken at uncertified schools "will not be accepted for licensing, reinstatement, or court requirements," and it asks you to verify a school's certification on its website before you enroll. A few signs that a course isn't what you need:

  • You can start any time and go at your own pace. A Georgia Risk Reduction class runs on a set schedule with a live instructor.
  • The price isn't $360. The cost is set by law, so every certified program charges the same. See our breakdown of what DUI school costs in Georgia.
  • There's no Georgia certification number. Georgia's rules require certified programs to show their certified name and certification number in their advertising.
  • The school isn't on the DDS list. DDS publishes a list of certified DUI schools. If the school isn't on it, don't pay.

Don't pay twice. A certificate from a self-paced online course won't reinstate your license. You'd still need to complete the full Risk Reduction Program at a certified school, and pay the $360 on top of what you already spent.

Does a live online class take less time?

No. The class is the same 20 hours whether you attend online or in person, spread over several sessions. DDS says that in most cases the whole course takes less than a week. Our page on how long DUI school takes in Georgia explains the scheduling rules.

What a live virtual class can save you is the drive. For many people that's the difference between fitting the class around work and missing a session.

Frequently asked questions

Does Georgia accept online DUI school?

Georgia does not accept self-paced online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses. DDS says certificates from those courses can't be used for anything related to your driver's license. A live class taught over video by a DDS-certified program is different, because Georgia's rules allow certified programs to teach remotely in real time.

Is a Zoom DUI class legit in Georgia?

It can be. Georgia's rules let a DDS-certified Risk Reduction Program teach through video services such as Zoom, as long as the class happens live at a scheduled time with a certified instructor. Check that the school is on the DDS list of certified DUI schools before you pay.

Can I take the DUI assessment online in Georgia?

Georgia's rules allow certified programs to give the assessment remotely, as long as they follow the DDS remote classroom guidelines. Each school decides how it runs the assessment, so ask your school whether you'll take it remotely or at its office.

Do I have to keep my camera on in an online DUI class?

Yes. DDS guidelines for remote classes say students must be visible on camera for every session, and audio alone is not acceptable. You also need to be logged in on time, seated and away from distractions.

Can I take a Georgia DUI class online if I live in another state?

Ask DDS before you enroll. If you have questions about whether an out-of-state program will be accepted, DDS suggests reviewing its DUI School Guidelines for Other States or calling the DDS Customer Contact Center at (678) 413-8400.
