What is a DUI clinical evaluation?

Georgia's rules define a clinical evaluation as the process used to diagnose a person's substance abuse or dependence and, if needed, refer them to the right treatment. It's run under the Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD), not DDS. DBHDD sets the qualifications for evaluators and treatment providers who work with DUI cases.

The evaluator must be a licensed or certified professional approved by DBHDD and listed on its registry of clinical evaluators. Evaluations can only take place at DBHDD-approved locations.

Who needs a clinical evaluation in Georgia?

There are two common reasons you'd need one.

SituationWhy an evaluation is needed
Two or more DUI convictions within 10 yearsDDS requires documented proof of a state-approved clinical evaluation before you can reinstate your license or driving privileges.
A court order or sentenceA judge can order an evaluation as part of a DUI sentence, including a first DUI. DBHDD says DUI offenders are required to attend DUI school, have a clinical evaluation and complete any treatment the evaluator recommends.

What to expect at a DUI evaluation

Georgia's rules set out what every DUI clinical evaluation includes. There are three parts.

  1. A clinical interview. This is a face-to-face conversation with the evaluator. The rules say it gathers your background, medical history, the alcohol concentration from your current offense, social and family history, substance use history, work background and mental status.
  2. A review of your DUI school screening. The evaluator looks at the standardized screening you completed for the Risk Reduction Program.
  3. At least one approved assessment tool. The evaluator uses one or more assessment instruments approved by DBHDD.

Honest answers help the evaluator place you correctly. The goal is to match you with the right level of service, or none at all if the evidence shows none is needed. It isn't a trial, and the evaluator isn't deciding your case.

What happens after the evaluation

Georgia's rules require the evaluator to complete a written report within seven days of your interview. The report shows what the evaluator recommends and why. From there, one of two things happens.

If treatment is recommended

The evaluator will recommend either short-term or longer-term treatment and give you a list of DBHDD-approved treatment providers for that level. You then need to complete that treatment. DBHDD's rules say:

  • You must stay in treatment for at least the time the evaluator recommended.
  • Longer-term treatment is at least three hours a week.
  • A client who follows the treatment plan can't be required to stay in treatment longer than one year.

Choose a provider from the DBHDD-approved list. Treatment from a provider that isn't approved for DUI cases may not count.

If no treatment is recommended

The evaluator sends the evaluation to DBHDD for review. DBHDD aims to finish its review within two weeks. If it approves the evaluation, DBHDD issues a Requirements Met Certificate directly to you. If it doesn't agree, it works with the evaluator on a treatment referral.
