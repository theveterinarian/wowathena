If you're under 21 in Georgia, driving with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of just 0.02 is a DUI. A first underage DUI suspends your license for 6 months if your BAC was under 0.08, or 12 months if it was 0.08 or higher or you refused testing, and no limited driving permit is available.

To get your license back, you'll need to finish DUI school (the Risk Reduction Program) and pay a reinstatement fee to the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS). Athens is a college town, so many people reading this are UGA students or their parents. This page covers the license side of an underage DUI. For your criminal case, talk to a DUI attorney. For any school discipline question, ask your school directly.

What is the legal limit for drivers under 21 in Georgia?

Georgia's DUI law, O.C.G.A. § 40-6-391(k), says a person under 21 may not drive with an alcohol concentration of 0.02 grams or more at any time within three hours after driving, from alcohol consumed before the driving ended. For drivers 21 and older, the limit is 0.08.

In practice, 0.02 can mean a single drink for some people. There's no amount you can count on being safe, so the only reliable rule is not to drink before you drive.

The 0.02 rule is on top of the regular DUI law, not instead of it. At any age, it's also illegal to drive while alcohol, a drug (prescription or illegal) or another substance makes you less safe to drive.

How long is your license suspended for a DUI under 21?

DDS counts offenses within a 5-year window, measured from arrest date to arrest date for arrests that led to convictions. Here's what the law and DDS publications say for drivers under 21.

Two details catch people off guard. First, DDS uses your age on the date of your conviction, not the date of your arrest, to decide which suspension rules apply. Second, a plea of nolo contendere doesn't avoid these suspensions. DDS treats it as a conviction.

Refusing the test doesn't help. On a first under-21 DUI, a refusal moves the suspension from 6 months to 12 months. Georgia's implied consent law also brings a separate suspension of at least one year if you refuse the state's chemical test.

Can you get a limited driving permit if you're under 21?

No. The DDS Driver's Manual lists DUI convictions before age 21 among the suspensions where a limited driving permit is not available. That means no permit to drive to work, class or a job site during the suspension.

This is one of the biggest differences from an adult DUI. A driver 21 or older with a first DUI may be able to get a limited permit. If you're under 21, plan on arranging rides, transit or a campus bus for the full 6 or 12 months. Our page on the Georgia limited driving permit after a DUI explains who does qualify.

How do you get your license back after an underage DUI?

Georgia law says a license suspended for a DUI under 21 stays suspended until you give DDS proof that you completed a DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program and pay the reinstatement fee. Here are the steps.

  1. Serve the full suspension. Unlike a first DUI at 21 or older, there's no option to apply early at 120 days. The suspension is 6, 12 or 18 months depending on your case.
  2. Complete a DDS-certified Risk Reduction Program. It costs $360 at every certified school and includes a 130-question assessment and a 20-hour course. DDS won't accept certificates from online DUI courses.
  3. Get a clinical evaluation if it applies to you. If you have two or more DUI convictions within 10 years, Georgia requires a clinical evaluation, and any treatment it recommends, before reinstatement.
  4. Pay the reinstatement fee. For a first under-21 suspension it's $210, or $200 by mail. For a second or later suspension it's $310, or $300 by mail.
  5. Check your record. Sign in to DDS Online Services to see your personal reinstatement requirements, or call DDS at 678-413-8400.

You don't have to wait until the end of your suspension to take DUI school. Finishing it early means your certificate is ready when you become eligible. See whether you can start DUI classes before your court date, and our full guide to getting your license back after a DUI in Georgia.

Put simply, the under-21 rules are stricter in two ways: the limit is much lower, and there's no permit and no early reinstatement on a first offense.

Other offenses that suspend a license for drivers under 21

Georgia's Teenage and Adult Driver Responsibility Act (TADRA) also suspends the license of a driver under 21 for several non-DUI convictions. According to DDS, these include:

  • Reckless driving or aggressive driving
  • Speeding 24 mph or more over the limit
  • Passing a stopped school bus, or improper passing on a hill or curve
  • Any other offense worth 4 or more points
  • Hit and run, racing, or fleeing or attempting to elude an officer
  • Four or more points in any 12 months while under 18

A first conviction brings a 6-month suspension and a second brings 12 months. To reinstate, DDS requires a DDS-approved defensive driving course, plus DUI school if the court ordered it, and the reinstatement fee. What UGA students and parents should know

  • Get legal advice early. DDS handles the license. The court handles the criminal case, and only an attorney can advise you on that. Georgia's DUI statute says a first or second under-21 conviction is a misdemeanor, and the court sets the sentence.
  • Out-of-state license? Georgia acts on your privilege to drive in Georgia. Check with DDS and with your home state's licensing agency about what happens to your license there.
  • Plan the class around your schedule. The 20-hour course usually takes less than a week. Live online classes work well around a class schedule, as long as they're taught live by a DDS-certified school.
  • Keep your paperwork. Hold on to your Risk Reduction certificate and any DDS notices. You'll need them to reinstate.
