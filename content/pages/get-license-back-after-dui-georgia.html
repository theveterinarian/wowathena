If you were 21 or older and this is your first DUI in 5 years, your Georgia license is suspended for 12 months, but you can apply to get it back after 120 days. To reinstate, you need a Certificate of Completion from a DDS-approved DUI Risk Reduction Program and the reinstatement fee, which is $200 by mail or $210 in person.

That's the short version. Below, we walk through each step in order, explain how to actually submit your reinstatement, and cover what changes if this is your second DUI, you were under 21, or you refused the state's breath or blood test.

How long is your license suspended after a DUI in Georgia?

It depends on your age, your record and whether you took the state's chemical test. This table shows the most common situations, based on the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS).

First DUI in 5 years, age 21 or older12 monthsAfter 120 days, with a Risk Reduction certificate and fee

Step by step: getting your license back after a first DUI

These steps apply to a first DUI in 5 years when you were 21 or older. The order matters less than finishing every step, because DDS won't reinstate you until everything is done.

  1. Check your record in DDS Online Services. DDS says the fastest way to see your requirements is to sign in to Online Services, which shows your license status and step-by-step reinstatement instructions based on your record. Give the court time to send your conviction to DDS before you check.
  2. Finish a DDS-approved Risk Reduction Program. This is Georgia DUI school: a screening assessment plus a 20-hour class, $360 at every certified school. DDS does not accept certificates from online DUI courses, so make sure your program is DDS-certified. Our complete guide to Georgia DUI school explains how it works.
  3. Serve the first 120 days. DDS lists "suspended for 120 days" as one of the requirements. You can't reinstate earlier than that, even if everything else is done.
  4. Have your insurer file an SR-22. A DDS rule that took effect in September 2025 (Rule 375-3-3-.24) says reinstating a license suspended for a DUI conviction requires an SR-22 insurance certificate, filed and kept for three years from the date of conviction.
  5. Pay the reinstatement fee and submit your certificate. The fee for a first DUI at 21 or older is $200 by mail or $210 in person. The next section explains each way to submit.
  6. Confirm you're reinstated. DDS says driving privileges are reinstated after your payment and all other requirements are processed, including any other suspensions on your record. Check your status in Online Services or the DDS 2 GO app before you drive.

Need to drive during the suspension? Many first-time DUI drivers 21 and older can get a limited driving permit for work, school, medical care and other approved trips. See our guide to the Georgia limited driving permit after a DUI.

When does the 120-day clock start?

This catches a lot of people off guard. According to the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual, DDS starts counting your reinstatement eligibility from whichever of these happens first:

  • The disposition date, if you surrendered your license (or signed a DDS lost license affidavit) at the time your case was decided.
  • The surrender date, if you turned in your license after your court date but before DDS processed the conviction.
  • The process date, the day DDS puts the conviction on your record, if you never surrendered your license.

In plain terms, surrendering your license promptly can keep your clock from starting late. The same manual says time you already served on an administrative suspension after your arrest counts toward the suspension for a DUI conviction from the same incident.

What changes after a second DUI within 5 years

A second DUI in 5 years is a much longer process. The DDS Driver's Manual says your license is suspended for at least 18 months, and for the first 120 days you have no driving privileges at all. After that "hard suspension," you may be able to drive with a certified ignition interlock device installed for 12 months.

You'll also need a clinical evaluation. DDS requires proof of a state-approved clinical evaluation to reinstate after two or more DUI convictions within 10 years, and you may need to complete treatment if the evaluation recommends it. Our guides to ignition interlock in Georgia and the clinical evaluation after a second DUI cover both in detail.

What changes if you were under 21

Georgia is stricter with drivers under 21. According to the DDS Driver's Manual:

  • If your alcohol level was .02 or more but under .08, or no test was given, the suspension is at least 6 months. It's at least 12 months if you have a prior conviction for certain offenses, such as reckless driving or speeding 24 mph or more over the limit.
  • If your alcohol level was .08 or more, or you refused testing, the suspension is at least 12 months.
  • No limited driving permit is available for a first DUI under 21.

You still need a Risk Reduction certificate to reinstate. Our page on Georgia DUI under 21 goes through the rules for younger drivers.

What changes if you refused the breath or blood test

Under Georgia's implied consent law, refusing the state's chemical test brings a license suspension of at least one year, separate from any DUI conviction. DDS also says a refusal suspension makes you ineligible for a regular limited driving permit if you're convicted of a first DUI.

Some drivers who refused may qualify for an ignition interlock limited permit instead. DDS lists that permit with a 12-month interlock requirement for drivers who did not consent to testing. Whether it's available to you depends on your record, so check with DDS.

Your record may not match the typical case. DDS says reinstatement requirements vary with the type of suspension, the conviction and whether you have more than one offense. Fees can also differ depending on your conviction date and other suspensions. Always check your own requirements in Online Services, or call DDS at 678-413-8400, Monday through Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m.

Common reasons reinstatement gets held up

  • A certificate DDS won't accept. Certificates from online DUI courses can't be used for anything related to your license. Only a DDS-certified program counts.
  • Another suspension on your record. DDS reinstates only after every outstanding suspension is handled, not just the DUI.
  • A missing SR-22. Ask your insurance company to file it before you try to reinstate.
  • A lost certificate. Your DUI school can issue a replacement for a fee of up to $20. If the school has closed, DDS can help at (678) 413-8745.
