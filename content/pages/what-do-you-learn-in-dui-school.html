In Georgia DUI school you take a private 130-question screening about your alcohol and drug use, then attend a 20-hour group class about alcohol, drugs and how they affect driving. Georgia's rules describe the class as "therapeutic education" plus "peer group counseling," and every certified school teaches the same DDS-approved curriculum, called Prime for Life.

The official name for DUI school is the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program. If you're nervous about it, that's normal. Knowing what each part involves takes a lot of the worry out of it.

What is DUI school?

DUI school is an intervention program required by Georgia law. The Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) says it's for people convicted of Driving Under the Influence, possession of illegal drugs, underage possession of alcohol while operating a motor vehicle, or Boating Under the Influence. If you're there for something other than a DUI, see Risk Reduction for drug possession, underage alcohol and BUI.

The program has two parts, an assessment and a class, and you have to finish both to get your Certificate of Completion. You'll need that certificate to reinstate your license after a DUI.

Part 1: the assessment

You complete the assessment before your class begins. DDS has approved one assessment for use in Georgia, the NEEDS Assessment. It's a 130-question "comprehensive screening instrument" that looks at how much you use alcohol and drugs and how that affects your driving.

A few things about it that people are often relieved to hear:

  • It's private. DDS policy says the assessment must be given in a way that affords you privacy and is free from distractions.
  • Your results are confidential. They may only be discussed with you, in private. They aren't shared with the class.
  • It's good for one year. If you don't enroll in the class within a year, you'll need to take a new assessment and pay the assessment fee again.
  • It can help later. If you ever need a clinical evaluation, the school must send your assessment results to the evaluator you choose within five business days of your request.

Part 2: the 20-hour class

The Intervention Component is the class itself. Georgia's rules define it as "therapeutic education about alcohol and drug use and driving, and peer group counseling concerning alcohol and drug use," given over at least 20 hours using a curriculum approved by DDS. DDS has approved one curriculum for this part, Prime for Life, from the Prevention Research Institute.

That means the class is the same at every certified school. The rules say course delivery, content and the order of instruction must follow the DDS-designated instructor's manual, and DDS policy says instructors must cover all the material in sequence, as the syllabus lays it out, without outside material. Every student also gets a new, unused copy of the workbook. It's part of the $360 price.

What topics does DUI school cover?

According to DDS, the class "covers alcohol, drug use, and their effects on driving." The detailed lesson plan belongs to the curriculum, which your instructor follows session by session, so we won't guess at it here. What the official descriptions tell you is the kind of learning to expect:

  • Education about alcohol and drugs. How alcohol and drug use affect you and, in particular, your driving.
  • Looking at your own choices. The assessment and the class are both built around your own alcohol and drug use and its impact on driving.
  • Learning from the group. Peer group counseling means you talk things through with the other students, guided by the instructor.

What does a DUI class day look like?

The class runs over several sessions, usually within a single week. DDS says that in most cases the whole 20-hour course takes less than a week. Here's what to expect from a typical session, based on the state rules and DDS policy:

  1. Arrive or log in early. Students who arrive late can expect to be turned away, and you may have to start the course over.
  2. Sign the class roll. Every student signs in at the beginning of each session.
  3. Work through the lesson with the group. Your instructor teaches the day's material, with videos, workbook activities and group discussion.
  4. Take breaks as scheduled. Breaks follow the course syllabus. If two sessions fall on the same day, there's a meal break of at least one hour between them, on top of the 20 hours.
  5. Stay to the end. No one may be admitted late or dismissed early from any session.

Classes are small enough for real discussion. The rules require at least five paid students for a class to be scheduled and cap classes at 40. DDS explains the minimum this way: the class requires a great deal of group interaction, which is hard to achieve with fewer than five people.

Do you have to participate?

Yes, you're expected to take part. Peer group counseling is part of the definition of the class, so plan to join the discussions and activities rather than sit in the back and wait for the clock. Your instructor will explain what's expected on the first day.

You won't be judged in there. DDS policy requires the class to be delivered "in a professional manner that is respectful, exhibits positive conduct and behavior, and is conducive to learning." Everyone in the room is there for a similar reason, and many people find the discussion more useful than they expected.

How to prepare for DUI school

  • Check every session date before you sign. DDS advises thinking about scheduling conflicts before you sign a contract, since missing a session can mean starting over.
  • Come sober. Instructors may not allow any student to attend while intoxicated or under the influence of an illegal or unauthorized substance.
  • Plan your day around the full session. Arrange work, childcare and transportation so you can stay until the end.
  • Ask your school what to bring. Your workbook is provided. Ask whether you need anything else, such as ID or court paperwork.
  • If you need help reading or writing, or have a disability, tell the school. Georgia's rules require reasonable accommodations.

If you're taking the class live online

Georgia's rules let certified schools teach the class live over video. DDS holds remote students to the same standards as a classroom. You'll need to be on camera for every session, logged in on time, seated in a quiet place away from distractions, and never driving. You can't record the class, and drinking, vaping and tobacco aren't allowed during sessions. Test your device and internet connection before the first session. What happens after DUI school?

At the end of the class your instructor gives a post-test and confirms you've met every course requirement. The school must then issue your Certificate of Completion within one business day, and no more than 48 hours, after you finish. You'll use it to reinstate your license, and you may need to show it to your court or probation officer.
