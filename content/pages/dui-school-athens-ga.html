The official name of DUI school is the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program. If a judge, a probation officer or the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) told you to complete "Risk Reduction" or "RRP," this is the course. We serve students from Athens-Clarke County and the surrounding Northeast Georgia counties.

What is the Risk Reduction Program?

DDS describes Risk Reduction as an intervention program required by law. It has two parts, and you must finish both to earn your Certificate of Completion.

  • The assessment. You do this first. It's a 130-question screening that looks at your alcohol and drug use and how it affects your driving.
  • The intervention course. This is the 20-hour class, taught over several sessions in a group. It covers alcohol, drugs and their effects on driving, with education and group discussion.

DDS says the whole 20-hour course takes less than a week in most cases.

Who needs DUI school in Georgia?

According to DDS, the program is for people convicted of any of these:

  • Driving Under the Influence (DUI)
  • Possession of illegal drugs
  • Underage possession of alcohol while operating a motor vehicle
  • Boating Under the Influence (BUI)

Many people are surprised that a drug charge or a boating charge can lead to the same class. We cover those situations in Risk Reduction for drug possession, underage alcohol and BUI. Your court order or DDS notice is the final word on what your case requires. If it's unclear, ask your attorney or the court.

Live online or in person: which counts?

Both of Athena's formats are the same certified program. You can sit in our classroom in Athens, or join the class live from home. Either way, an instructor teaches the group in real time, and you earn the same certificate.

The key word is live. Georgia's DDS rules let certified programs teach through platforms like Zoom, as long as the instruction is synchronous: real-time teaching and interaction, on a specific platform, at a specific time. That is different from a self-paced online course you click through alone.

Self-paced online DUI courses won't work for your license. DDS says certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses cannot be accepted for any purpose related to your driver's license or driving privileges. If a website offers a DUI class you can finish on your own time, or for less than $360, it isn't a Georgia-certified Risk Reduction Program.

What to expect in class

The course is taught in a group, and DDS rules require a minimum of five paid students for a scheduled class. The rules also let a school cancel, reschedule or go ahead if fewer than five show up. If that ever affects your class, call us and we'll help you find the next date.

Attendance rules are strict at every certified school. DDS warns that students who arrive late can expect to be denied entry, and students who miss a session or leave early may have to start over at full cost. For live online classes, plan to be in a quiet spot with a working connection before class begins.

Your certificate and getting your license back

Your Certificate of Completion is what DDS asks for when you reinstate. For a first DUI in five years at age 21 or older, DDS suspends the license for 12 months, but you can apply to reinstate after 120 days with your certificate and the reinstatement fee. That fee is $200 by mail or $210 in person, and it's paid to DDS, not to the school.

Other offenses and repeat offenses have different rules. After two or more DUI convictions within 10 years, for example, DDS also requires proof of a clinical evaluation. Our license reinstatement guide walks through each case.

Keep your certificate safe. DDS says a school can charge up to $20 for a replacement, and schools only have to keep student records for three years.
