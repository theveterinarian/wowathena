The official name of DUI school is the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program. If a judge, a probation officer or the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) told you to complete "Risk Reduction" or "RRP," this is the course, and this page takes you from the court order to the day you reinstate.

What is the Risk Reduction Program?

DDS describes Risk Reduction as an intervention program required by law, with two parts you must finish to earn your Certificate of Completion.

  • The assessment. A 130-question screening, called the NEEDS assessment, that looks at your alcohol and drug use and how it affects your driving. You do this first.
  • The intervention course. The 20-hour class, taught over several sessions in a group using the DDS-approved Prime for Life curriculum. It covers alcohol, drugs and their effects on driving.

DDS says the whole 20-hour course takes less than a week in most cases (see how long DUI school takes in Georgia).

Who needs DUI school in Georgia?

According to DDS, the program is for people convicted of any of these:

  • Driving Under the Influence (DUI)
  • Possession of illegal drugs
  • Underage possession of alcohol while operating a motor vehicle
  • Boating Under the Influence (BUI)

We cover the non-DUI cases in Risk Reduction for drug possession, underage alcohol and BUI. Your court order or DDS record is the final word on what your case requires.

From court order to reinstatement: step by step

Here is the path for the most common case, a first DUI at age 21 or older. Other situations are covered further down.

  1. Read your sentence and your DDS notice. Your court paperwork says whether DUI school is a condition of your sentence and any deadline. Keep the Official Notice of Suspension DDS sends; you'll need it to reinstate.
  2. Check your record in DDS Online Services. DDS says the fastest way to see your exact reinstatement steps is to sign in to Online Services or the DDS 2 GO app, once the court has reported the conviction.
  3. Register with a certified school. DDS only accepts certificates from schools on its certified list; ours is RRP Cert #10432. Under DDS Rule 375-3-3-.05 the course only counts if it was taken and completed after the violation date.
  4. Sign your contracts. DDS rules require a written contract for each component, with a copy given to you before any service begins.
  5. Complete the assessment. It's processed at least 30 minutes before your first session. Under DDS policy an assessment is valid for one year; start the course within that year or you pay for a new one.
  6. Attend all 20 hours. DDS Rule 375-5-6-.15 requires the school to schedule every session within four weeks, with no more than two sessions in one day. You sign the class roll at the start of each session.
  7. Receive your Certificate of Completion. Keep the original. When needed, we send a copy to your court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost.
  8. Wait out the 120 days, then apply to reinstate. For a first DUI in five years at age 21 or older, DDS suspends the license for 12 months but lets you apply after 120 days. Mail your original certificate, the Official Notice of Suspension and full payment ($200) to DDS Validation Unit, P.O. Box 80447, Conyers, Georgia 30013, or bring them to a Customer Service Center and pay $210. DDS says partial payments are not accepted.
  9. Keep your SR-22 on file. Since September 2025, DDS Rule 375-3-3-.24 requires an SR-22 insurance certificate filed and maintained for three years from the conviction date to reinstate a DUI suspension. Your insurer files it.

The four-week window and the attendance rules

DDS Rule 375-5-6-.15 sets the rules every certified school must follow for the 20-hour course.

  • Four weeks, two sessions a day at most. All sessions must be completed within four weeks, with no more than two sessions in a single day.
  • All 20 hours, no exceptions. No student may be admitted late or dismissed early from any session.
  • Class size. A course needs at least five paid contracts and no more than 40 students. If fewer than five show up for the first session, the school may teach, cancel or reschedule.
  • Sign in every time. You sign a DDS-format class roll at the start of each session.
  • Accommodations. Schools must provide reasonable accommodations for students who are disabled or need help reading or writing.

What happens if you miss a session

DDS's FAQ is blunt: students who arrive late can expect to be denied entry, and students who are absent or leave early may need to start over at full cost. Refunds and make-ups are between you and the school under your contract.

The rule does allow make-ups for an excused absence: military duty, a personal medical emergency or one involving an immediate family member, or a death in your immediate family. Other absences may be excused at the owner's discretion, and a student with an excused absence may finish the missed sessions in another course. Call us before the session, not after.

Live online or in person: which counts?

Both of Athena's formats are the same certified program. DDS Rule 375-5-6-.18 lets certified programs teach through platforms like Zoom as long as the instruction is synchronous: real-time teaching and interaction, on a specific platform, at a specific time, with DDS notified at least 24 hours before class. Online, be settled with a working connection before class starts; the late-arrival rule still applies.

Self-paced online DUI courses won't work for your license. DDS says certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses cannot be accepted for any purpose related to your driver's license or driving privileges. If a website offers a DUI class you can finish on your own time, or for less than $360, it isn't a Georgia-certified Risk Reduction Program. More in Can you take DUI school online in Georgia?

What to bring, and what to keep

  • To reinstate: your original Certificate of Completion, the Official Notice of Suspension, full payment and, in person, proof of identity. If you never received a notice, DDS asks for your full name, date of birth, license number, address and Social Security number.
  • Keep for years: your certificate, contracts, DDS receipt and SR-22 confirmation. Schools only keep records for three years, and a replacement certificate costs up to $20.

Special situations

Under 21

Georgia presumes a driver under 21 is DUI at a blood alcohol concentration of .02 or more. DDS's Driver's Manual says a first suspension is at least six months for a BAC under .08, and at least 12 months for .08 or higher or a refusal, with no limited permit either way. See Georgia DUI under 21.

Second DUI in five years

DDS says a second DUI conviction within five years brings a suspension of at least 18 months, with no driving at all for the first 120 days. After that "hard suspension" you may be eligible for an ignition interlock limited permit, which under Rule 375-3-3-.10 requires proof of the installed device, your Risk Reduction certificate and court authorization. Two or more DUI convictions within ten years also require a clinical evaluation to reinstate. See ignition interlock in Georgia.

You refused the test

A refusal suspension runs at least one year on its own, and DDS says it makes you ineligible for a limited permit if you're convicted of a first DUI. DUI school is still required.

Out-of-state license or out-of-state DUI

DDS says that if your privileges are suspended in another state for a DUI, you can't get a Georgia license until that state's requirements are met. DDS publishes DUI school guidelines by state and suggests calling (678) 413-8400 before you enroll in a class for another state.

Court-ordered vs. DDS-required

DDS's DUI chart notes that a court may order a clinical evaluation on a first DUI even though DDS doesn't require one, and your judge may set a DUI school deadline earlier than DDS does. Follow the stricter one.

Key terms

  • Risk Reduction Program: Georgia's legal name for DUI school; an assessment plus a 20-hour intervention course.
  • NEEDS assessment: the 130-question screening DDS has approved as the assessment component.
  • Certificate of Completion: the document a certified school issues once both components are done; DDS requires the original to reinstate.
  • Limited driving permit: a restricted permit DDS may issue during a suspension for driving to work, school, medical care, treatment and court.
  • Hard suspension: DDS's term for the first 120 days of a second-DUI suspension, when no driving privileges are available.
  • Ignition interlock: a device that keeps a vehicle from starting at a blood alcohol concentration above 0.02; required for 12 months after a second DUI in five years.
  • Implied consent: Georgia's rule that drivers must submit to state chemical tests; refusal brings a suspension of at least one year.
  • SR-22: an insurance certificate your insurer files with DDS; required for three years from a DUI conviction.
