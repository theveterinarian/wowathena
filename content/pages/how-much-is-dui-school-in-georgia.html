DUI school in Georgia costs $360. That's the price at every program certified by the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS), whether you take it in Athens, Atlanta or Savannah, live online or in person. The state sets the amount, so no certified school can charge you more or less.

The official name for DUI school is the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program, often shortened to "Risk Reduction" or "RRP." If a judge or DDS has told you to complete it, this is the class they mean.

Where the $360 goes

DDS breaks the price into three parts. You pay for all three, and you need all three to earn your Certificate of Completion.

Why every school charges the same price

Because DDS sets the price, shopping around won't save you money. What does change from school to school is everything else: the class schedule, whether you can attend live online or in person, how easy registration is, and whether you can reach a real person when you have a question about your court order.

What the fee rule says

The price is fixed by law and by DDS Rule 375-5-6-.26. That rule says fees must follow O.C.G.A. 40-5-83(e), that the materials fee set by DDS is not optional, and that no program owner, instructor or employee may offer any component free of charge or charge a reduced or inflated fee. A companion rule, 375-5-6-.25, bars schools from advertising that your license will be reinstated or that free or reduced fees will be given, and requires every ad to show the school's certified name and certification number. So a school that advertises a discount, or doesn't show a certification number, is a warning sign.

When you pay, and what you get

Rule 375-5-6-.26 says fees for the assessment and the class are paid when you sign the contract, and DDS policy adds that you can't complete the assessment until the assessment fee is paid in full. Rule 375-5-6-.16 requires the school to give you a copy of each contract before any service is delivered. Read it: DDS says the contract holds the school's attendance and refund policies.

The rules protect you too. Rule 375-5-6-.23 requires the school to issue your certificate within one business day, and no more than 48 hours, after you finish, and Rule 375-5-6-.26 says a school may not withhold a certificate from a student who completed all requirements because of unpaid course fees.

Watch out for "cheap" online DUI classes. The Georgia DDS says certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses can't be accepted for anything related to your driver's license. If a website offers a self-paced online DUI class for less than $360, it isn't a Georgia-certified Risk Reduction Program. Live virtual classes taught in real time by a certified school are a different thing, and they cost the same $360.

Refunds, transfers and missed classes

This is where people lose money, so it's worth knowing the rules before you sign.

Missed or late sessions

DDS says every school has strict attendance policies, and that they're in the contract you sign. Students who arrive late can expect to be denied entry, and being absent or leaving early may mean starting the course over at full cost. Rule 375-5-6-.15 says you must attend the entire 20 hours and no student may be admitted late or dismissed early, but a student with an excused absence (military duty, a medical emergency, a death in the immediate family, or anything else the owner excuses) may finish the missed sessions in another class. DDS says whether you get a refund or a makeup is between you and the school.

Transfers to another school

DDS allows you to transfer your assessment results to another school in three situations: the class you contracted for was cancelled, you moved at least 30 miles from the school, or there's an emergency and DDS approved it in advance. The process is to confirm a seat at the new school, get verbal approval from DDS at 678-413-8745 (8 a.m. to 4 p.m., Monday through Friday, excluding state holidays), then complete the Authorization for the Transfer and/or Release of Assessment Results form. The school may charge up to $25 for a transfer due to relocation. DDS's policy guide says a transfer fee may not be charged when the class was cancelled.

When the rules require a refund

Two DDS rules spell out refunds. If a school relocates 10 or more miles, Rule 375-5-6-.18 says students with a valid assessment who haven't finished the class may cancel and receive a full refund or transfer to another school at no charge. If a school permanently closes, Rule 375-5-6-.21 gives the same choice. Outside those cases, refunds follow your contract.

Class size and cancellations

Rule 375-5-6-.15 requires at least five paid contracts for a scheduled class and lets the school cancel, reschedule or go ahead if fewer than five students show up. A cancelled class is one of the situations where a free transfer is allowed.

Assessments expire

DDS policy says the assessment is valid for one year. If you don't enroll in the class within that year, you'll need a new assessment and pay the $100 assessment fee again. A new conviction also requires a new assessment.

Common mistakes that cost money

  • Paying for an online DUI course. DDS won't accept the certificate, so you'll pay $360 again for a certified class.
  • Taking the class before the violation date. Rule 375-3-3-.05 says a certificate only counts for reinstatement if the course was taken and completed after the violation date. Taking it early to "get ahead" means paying twice.
  • Not checking certification. DDS says classes at uncertified schools won't be accepted for licensing, reinstatement or court requirements. Check the school on DDS's certified DUI schools list and look for its certification number in its ads.
  • Arriving late. DDS says late students can expect to be turned away, and a missed session may mean restarting at full cost.
  • Letting the assessment lapse. Wait more than a year to start class and you pay another $100.
  • Losing the certificate. A replacement costs up to $20, and schools only keep records for three years. Keep a copy.

Questions to ask before you pay

  • The school: What's your DDS certification number? Is the class live online or in person? When does it start and finish? What's the attendance and makeup policy? Will you send my certificate to the court?
  • The clerk or your probation officer: Is there a deadline to show proof of DUI school? Where should the certificate go?
  • DDS (678-413-8400): What does my record show as my reinstatement requirements and fee? Am I eligible for a limited permit?
  • Your insurer: What will an SR-22 filing cost?

Key terms

  • Risk Reduction Program (RRP). Georgia's official name for DUI school: an assessment plus a 20-hour class, offered only by DDS-certified programs.
  • NEEDS assessment. The 130-question screening DDS has approved as the assessment component. It's valid for one year.
  • Intervention component. The 20-hour class, using the DDS-approved Prime for Life curriculum.
  • Certificate of Completion. The document the school issues within 48 hours of finishing both components. You need it to reinstate.
  • Reinstatement fee. The fee paid to DDS to restore your license: $200 by mail or $210 in person for a first DUI at 21 or older.
  • Limited driving permit. A $32 DDS permit that lets an eligible driver drive for limited purposes, such as work and school, during a suspension.
  • Ignition interlock device limited permit. A $25 DDS permit tied to an interlock device, required for 12 months after a second DUI in five years.
  • Clinical evaluation. A substance use evaluation by a DBHDD-approved evaluator, required for reinstatement after two or more DUIs in 10 years.
  • SR-22. A certificate from your insurer proving liability coverage, required for three years after a DUI conviction under Rule 375-3-3-.24.
  • Nolo contendere. A "no contest" plea. The DDS Driver's Manual says it counts as a conviction for suspensions that apply to drivers under 21.
