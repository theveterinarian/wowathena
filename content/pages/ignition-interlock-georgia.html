Georgia requires an ignition interlock device mainly after a second DUI within 5 years. After a 120-day suspension with no driving at all, you may be able to drive again, but only with an interlock device installed for 12 months and an ignition interlock limited permit from the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS).

Interlock permits also come up after an arrest, when you can choose one instead of fighting the administrative suspension, and after a third DUI. This guide explains each situation, the permit requirements, the hardship waiver and the costs that official sources actually state.

What is an ignition interlock device?

DDS describes an ignition interlock device (IID) as a breath alcohol analyzer connected to your vehicle's ignition. You blow into it before driving, and if your breath alcohol is over the preset limit, the vehicle won't start. Under DDS rules, the device is designed to stop you from driving with an alcohol concentration of more than 0.02.

DDS certifies and regulates the provider centers that install, maintain, recalibrate and remove these devices in Georgia. DDS publishes a list of certified ignition interlock providers on its website, and it says taking part in the program requires a Georgia limited permit.

The ignition interlock permit after a second DUI

DDS Rule 375-3-3-.10 lists what you must give DDS to get an interlock permit after a second DUI conviction within 5 years:

  1. Proof of installation of an approved ignition interlock device on any vehicle you'll drive with the permit.
  2. A certificate of completion from a DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program.
  3. Authorization from the court that handled your DUI, and enrollment in a drug court or in clinical treatment that meets Georgia's requirements.

The DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual describes the same steps. It says you need proof of enrollment in, or completion of, a treatment program approved by the Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD) if your clinical evaluation requires treatment, or permission from the court if your case is in a DUI court program.

After you keep the device for the required time, the rule says you become eligible for a permit without the interlock restriction, if you're otherwise eligible. The court manual describes the full sequence as 120 days of hard suspension, 12 months with the interlock, and a regular limited permit for the rest of the suspension.

The interlock permit after an arrest

After a DUI arrest, DDS can suspend your license administratively, separate from your court case. According to the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual, you can request an ignition interlock limited permit instead of an administrative hearing if you:

  • Are 21 or older and hold a Georgia license.
  • Aren't facing an administrative suspension for a crash that involved injuries or deaths.
  • Have no other active suspensions, cancellations, denials or revocations.

The manual says the request must be made within 30 days of being served with the suspension notice (DDS Form 1205), and that getting the permit waives your right to the administrative hearing. CDL holders lose commercial privileges for the length of the suspension.

If you refused the test, this choice sticks. The court manual says that if you take the interlock permit for a refusal suspension, you must keep it for the full monitoring period even if your DUI charge is later dismissed or reduced. Choosing between the permit and a hearing is a legal decision. Talk to your attorney before you decide.

How long do you need the interlock device?

For a second DUI in 5 years, the time depends on the date of the incident that led to the conviction. DDS Rule 375-3-3-.10 sets it out this way:

Incident dateInterlock requirement
Before January 1, 20136 months
January 1, 2013 to June 30, 20138 months
On or after July 1, 201312 months

When your time is up, the device doesn't come off on its own schedule. DDS rules require a provider to see proof that DDS or the court has authorized removal before taking the device out. DDS charges a $100 fee to remove the interlock restriction from your permit, on top of any reinstatement fees.

Is there an ignition interlock waiver for financial hardship?

Yes, but it comes with a cost in time. A sentencing court can exempt a person from the interlock requirement because of financial hardship under O.C.G.A. § 42-8-111(a)(1). DDS Rule 375-3-3-.10 says a person with that exemption can't get any limited permit until they've served at least 12 months of the suspension. The DDS court manual describes this as a 12-month hard suspension.

The waiver is granted by the court, not by DDS or your DUI school. If you think you may qualify, raise it with your attorney before sentencing.

How Risk Reduction and the clinical evaluation fit in

An interlock permit doesn't replace the other requirements after a second DUI. It sits alongside them:

  • Risk Reduction Program. You need the certificate before DDS will issue the interlock permit after a second DUI. This is the same 20-hour DDS-certified DUI school, $360. See our Georgia DUI school guide.
  • Clinical evaluation. DDS requires proof of a state-approved clinical evaluation to reinstate after two or more DUI convictions in 10 years. Read more in Second DUI in Georgia: the clinical evaluation.
  • Treatment, if recommended. The court manual says you'll need proof of completing treatment if your evaluation requires it, or a "Requirements Met" certificate if no treatment was recommended.

Rules for driving with an interlock

The provider must inspect and recalibrate your device every 30 days, and the interlock permit lets you drive to those monthly monitoring visits. Tampering is treated seriously. The warning label DDS requires on every device says tampering or letting someone else tamper is a misdemeanor, and providers must report signs of tampering in writing within 5 days.

According to the DDS court manual, DDS revokes the permit if a provider reports tampering, a missed monitoring visit or early removal of the device, or if you're convicted of a moving violation or of violating the permit's restrictions. After a revocation, you can't apply for reinstatement for 6 months.
