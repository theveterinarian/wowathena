A Georgia limited driving permit lets you drive while your license is suspended, but only for approved reasons like getting to work, school, scheduled medical care and court. After a DUI, you can usually get one if it's your first DUI in 5 years, you were 21 or older, and you did not refuse the state's breath or blood test.

The permit costs $32 and lasts up to one year, or until you reinstate your license, whichever comes first. It's issued by the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS). This guide covers who qualifies, what the permit allows, what disqualifies you and how to apply.

What is a limited permit in Georgia?

A limited permit is a restricted license you can hold during a suspension. It doesn't give your full driving privileges back. Instead, DDS prints the approved uses as restrictions on the permit, and you can drive only for those reasons.

People often call it a "work permit" or "hardship license." Georgia law, O.C.G.A. § 40-5-64, is the statute behind it. According to the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual, DDS issues the permit when your application shows that refusing it would cause you extreme hardship.

What a limited driving permit lets you do

DDS lists these as the purposes a limited permit can cover:

  • Driving to your place of employment and performing the normal duties of your job.
  • Getting scheduled medical care or picking up prescription drugs.
  • Attending classes at a college or school where you're enrolled as a student.
  • Attending support group meetings for the treatment of alcohol or other drugs.
  • Attending a driver education program or an alcohol or drug assessment and treatment program.
  • Attending court, reporting to community supervision or probation, or doing community service.
  • Driving unlicensed immediate family members to work, school, medical care or to get prescriptions.
  • Attending programs ordered by an accountability court judge.

DDS can add limits on top of those, including the places you can go, the routes you can take, the times you can drive and the vehicles you can use. A limited permit is never valid for driving a commercial vehicle.

Who qualifies for a limited permit after a DUI?

For a DUI suspension, the regular limited permit is meant mainly for first offenses. Based on DDS's pages and rules, you generally need to meet all of these:

  • No other DUI conviction within 5 years, measured from the date of the earlier arrest to the date of the current arrest.
  • Age 21 or older. The DDS Driver's Manual lists DUI convictions before age 21 as not eligible for a limited permit.
  • No refusal suspension. DDS says refusing the state's chemical test makes you ineligible for a limited permit if you're convicted of a first DUI.
  • A Georgia resident who has held a Georgia license. DDS Rule 375-3-3-.10 says non-residents and people who've never had a Georgia license aren't eligible.
  • No other suspensions. The same rule excludes people whose license is withdrawn for another reason, unless they're separately eligible for a permit for that suspension too.

How to apply for a limited driving permit in Georgia

You can apply for the permit right after your conviction, as long as your license has been surrendered. Here's how the process works, based on DDS's rule and forms:

  1. Get the referral form from the court. At conviction, the court can complete DDS Form 1126, the First DUI Conviction Permit Referral. DDS doesn't need it if your DUI conviction is already on your driving record.
  2. Surrender your license. DDS requires you to turn in your license, or sign a lost license affidavit (Form DS-250A) if you can't.
  3. Complete the permit application. The DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual says applications are made on DDS forms and must be signed before a person authorized to give oaths, such as a notary.
  4. Take it to a DDS Customer Service Center and pay the $32 fee. The court manual tells courts to send drivers with the DDS-1126 form to the nearest Customer Service Center for the permit.

The referral isn't approval. The DDS-1126 form itself says a court referral doesn't mean a permit will be issued. A refusal suspension, another suspension or a prior conviction can still rule you out. Call DDS at 678-413-8400 if you're unsure before making the trip.

Is there a deadline to finish DUI school while on a permit?

The DDS pages and rule we reviewed don't list a separate deadline for finishing the Risk Reduction Program in order to keep a first-offense limited permit. What they do say is that you need the Risk Reduction certificate to reinstate your license. If your court order sets its own deadline for DUI school, follow the court order. If you're unsure, ask DDS or your attorney.

The approved uses include driving to a driver education program or an alcohol or drug assessment and treatment program. Check the restrictions printed on your own permit before you drive to class. What can get your limited permit revoked?

DDS will revoke your permit if:

  • You're convicted of breaking any state law or local ordinance about the movement of vehicles, such as a speeding ticket.
  • You're convicted of violating the conditions or restrictions on your permit.
  • Another suspension is imposed.

The penalties are steep. The DDS Driver's Manual says a conviction extends your underlying suspension by 6 months, and the Traffic Court Reference Manual says you can't apply for a license until 6 months after the permit is surrendered or DDS processes the conviction. Stick strictly to the purposes and limits printed on your permit.

Other permits: before court, and after a second DUI

Before your court date. After a DUI arrest, you may face an administrative license suspension from DDS. The DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual says that if you took the test and this is your first administrative suspension, a limited permit may be available, and it can carry over as your DUI permit if you're convicted at age 21 or older.

Ignition interlock permits. After a second DUI in 5 years, or in some refusal cases, the option is an ignition interlock limited permit, which requires a breath-testing device in your vehicle. Those permits have different rules, fees and time limits. See our guide to ignition interlock in Georgia.

Under 21? The rules are stricter, and no limited permit is available for a first DUI. Our page on Georgia DUI under 21 explains what to expect.
