Georgia DUI school is the state's DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program: a 130-question assessment followed by a 20-hour class, taught only by programs certified by the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS). It costs $360 everywhere in Georgia, most people finish in less than a week, and the Certificate of Completion you earn is what DDS needs to reinstate your license after a DUI.

If you've just been arrested, convicted or handed a court order, this guide covers the whole program in one place. Each section gives you the short answer and links to a page that goes deeper.

What is Georgia DUI school?

"DUI school" is the everyday name. The official name is the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program, and you'll also hear it called "Risk Reduction," "RRP" or "DUI classes." DDS describes it as an intervention program mandated by law. It provides a standardized screening of your alcohol and drug use along with a DDS-approved therapeutic education course focused on alcohol, drug use and driving.

DDS regulates the program through its Regulatory Compliance Division, under the rules in Chapter 375-5-6 of the Georgia rules and regulations. Those rules decide who can teach, how the class is scheduled, how certificates are issued, and what schools can and can't say in their advertising. Because the rules and the curriculum are the same statewide, the class you take in Athens is the same class someone takes in Savannah.

Only DDS-certified programs can teach it. DDS says classes taken at uncertified schools will not be accepted for licensing, reinstatement or court requirements.

Who has to take DUI school in Georgia?

DDS says the program is for people convicted of:

  • Driving Under the Influence (DUI)
  • Possession of illegal drugs
  • Underage possession of alcohol while operating a motor vehicle
  • Boating Under the Influence (BUI)

Many people are surprised by the last three. A drug possession conviction can require DUI school too, and so can a BUI conviction from a day on the lake. We explain those situations in Not just DUI: drug possession, underage alcohol and BUI.

Your court may also list DUI school as part of your sentence. If you have a court order, it's the first thing to check: what it requires, and by when. If the wording isn't clear, ask your attorney or the court clerk. Drivers under 21 face their own set of rules, covered further down.

How DUI school works: the assessment and the 20-hour class

The program has two parts, and you must complete both to receive a Certificate of Completion.

PartWhat happensTimeCost
Assessment ComponentThe NEEDS Assessment, a 130-question screening of your alcohol and drug use and how it affects your drivingDone first, before class starts$100
Intervention ComponentTherapeutic education and peer group counseling, using the DDS-approved Prime for Life curriculum20 hours over several sessions$235
WorkbookA new, unused workbook for each studentUsed during class$25

The assessment is individual and private. DDS policy says your results are confidential and may only be discussed with you in private. The assessment is valid for one year, so if you don't enroll in the class within a year, you'll have to take it again and pay again.

The class is taught in a group by a DDS-certified instructor, following the DDS-designated instructor's manual in order. Georgia's rules define it as "therapeutic education about alcohol and drug use and driving, and peer group counseling concerning alcohol and drug use." Expect group discussion and workbook activities, not just lectures. At the end, your instructor gives a post-test before your certificate is issued.

If your class is cancelled or you move

A class needs enough students to run. Georgia's rules require at least five paid contracts for a scheduled class, and if fewer than five students show up for the first session, the school may go ahead, cancel or reschedule. DDS explains that the class depends on group interaction, which is hard with fewer than five people.

Your assessment can move with you in limited cases. DDS allows a transfer of your assessment results to another school if your contracted class was cancelled, if you moved at least 30 miles away from the school, or in an emergency with DDS approval. You need verbal approval from DDS at (678) 413-8745 first, and then a signed transfer form. A school may charge up to $25 to transfer results when you move, but DDS policy says no transfer fee can be charged when the school cancelled your class.

If you need accommodations

Georgia's rules require schools to provide reasonable accommodations for students who have a disability or who need help reading or writing. Tell the school when you register so it can plan ahead. That applies to live virtual classes too: DDS guidelines allow exceptions to the "contracted students only" rule for students who need that kind of help.

How much does DUI school cost in Georgia?

DUI school costs $360 at every certified program: $100 for the assessment, $235 for the class and $25 for the workbook. DDS says the cost is set by law, and Georgia's rules forbid programs from offering any part free or charging a reduced or inflated fee. So you can't shop around for a lower price, and you shouldn't be charged more.

What the $360 doesn't cover is your license reinstatement fee, which you pay to DDS. For a first DUI at 21 or older that's $200 by mail or online, or $210 in person. A clinical evaluation, if you need one, is also separate. See the full breakdown in How much is DUI school in Georgia?

How long does DUI school take?

The class is 20 hours, plus the assessment you take beforehand. DDS says that in most cases the whole course takes less than a week, depending on the school's schedule.

Georgia's rules set the limits every school works within:

  • All sessions must be completed within four weeks.
  • No more than two sessions can be held in one day, with a meal break of at least an hour between them that doesn't count toward the 20 hours.
  • You must attend the entire 20 hours. No one may be admitted late or dismissed early from any session.
  • Sessions are taken in order. DDS policy says you can't attend the next session after missing one.

Attendance is where people lose time. DDS warns that late students can expect to be turned away, and anyone absent or leaving early may have to start over at full cost. The rules do let you make up sessions in another class after an excused absence, such as military duty, a medical emergency or a death in your immediate family. Details are in How long is DUI school in Georgia?

Can you take DUI school online in Georgia?

Not as a self-paced course. DDS says certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses "cannot be accepted by the DDS for any purposes related to an individual's driver's license or driving privileges."

Live virtual classes are different. Georgia Rule 375-5-6-.18 allows a certified program to "utilize remote services for classroom instruction and the administration of assessments," through services such as Zoom, as long as instruction is "synchronous allowing for real-time instruction and interaction" at a specific time.

Your Certificate of Completion and getting your license back

When you finish all program requirements, Georgia's rules say the school must issue your Certificate of Completion within one business day, and no more than 48 hours. No certificate can be issued before you complete the class. Keep it safe: a replacement from the school costs up to $20, and schools only have to keep student records for three years.

If the school where you took the class later closes, DDS can issue a replacement. Call DDS at (678) 413-8745 with the school's name and your course dates. And if a school announces it's closing before you've finished, Georgia's rules let students with a valid assessment who haven't completed the class cancel for a full refund or transfer to another program at no charge.

For a first DUI in five years at age 21 or older, DDS suspends your license for 12 months. After 120 days you can apply for reinstatement if you present your Risk Reduction Certificate of Completion and pay the reinstatement fee. Since the class usually takes less than a week, finishing it early means you're ready to apply when you become eligible. If you refused the state's chemical test, DDS says your suspension is at least one year.

Reinstatement rules vary with your age, your record and whether you refused testing. Our guide to getting your license back after a DUI in Georgia walks through each case step by step.

After a DUI in Georgia: the other steps you may face

DUI school is usually one of several requirements. Here's a short look at the others, with a link to the full guide for each.

Second DUI within 10 years: the clinical evaluation

DDS requires proof of a state-approved clinical evaluation to reinstate a license suspended for two or more DUI convictions within 10 years. Clinical evaluations are regulated by the Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD), not DDS, and the evaluation may recommend treatment. Your DUI school assessment can help: DDS policy says the school must send your assessment results to the clinical evaluator you choose within five business days of your request. Read Second DUI in Georgia: the clinical evaluation, or see our clinical evaluation in Athens page.

Limited driving permit

Some drivers can get a limited driving permit during a suspension. DDS says a permit is "possibly" available after a first DUI, but refusing the state's chemical test makes you ineligible if you're convicted of a first DUI. Whether you qualify depends on your situation. See Georgia limited driving permit after a DUI.

Ignition interlock

According to the DDS Driver's Manual, a second DUI within five years brings a suspension of at least 18 months, with no driving at all for the first 120 days. After that you may be eligible to drive with a certified ignition interlock device installed for 12 months. Learn more in Ignition interlock in Georgia.

DUI under 21

In Georgia, drivers under 21 are presumed to be DUI with a blood alcohol concentration of .02 or more. The Driver's Manual says a first suspension lasts at least 6 months for a BAC from .02 up to .08, or at least 12 months for .08 or more or a test refusal, with no limited permit in either case. See Georgia DUI under 21.

How to choose a DDS-certified DUI school

Price won't separate one school from another, because it's $360 everywhere. These things will:

  • Certification. Check the DDS list of certified DUI schools before you pay. Georgia's rules require certified programs to show their certified name and certification number in their ads, so look for it.
  • No promises about your license. The rules forbid programs from advertising that you're certain to get your license reinstated. Be wary of anyone who says otherwise.
  • A schedule you can keep. Look at every session date and time before you sign. Missing a session can mean starting over.
  • The format you need. Ask whether the school teaches live virtual classes, in-person classes or both.
  • Someone who answers the phone. Georgia's rules require programs to keep at least 15 business hours a week, with staff available to answer the phone and give information about the program.
  • Help with paperwork. Ask whether the school will send your certificate to your court or probation officer if you need it to.

If your license is suspended in another state because of a DUI conviction, DDS says you can't get a Georgia license until you've met that state's reinstatement requirements. If you have a question about an out-of-state program, DDS suggests checking its DUI School Guidelines for Other States or calling the DDS Customer Contact Center at (678) 413-8400 before you enroll.

If something goes wrong with a school, an instructor or a class, DDS takes complaints in writing on its official complaint form.

What to do next: a step-by-step plan

  1. Read your paperwork. Find out what your court order or DDS notice requires, and any deadlines. If anything is unclear, ask your attorney, the court or DDS.
  2. Work out which extra steps apply. Check whether you need a clinical evaluation (two or more DUIs in 10 years), whether you might qualify for a limited permit, and whether ignition interlock applies to you.
  3. Choose a DDS-certified program. Confirm it's on the DDS list and pick a schedule and format that fit your week.
  4. Register and complete the assessment. You'll sign contracts for the assessment and the class and take the 130-question screening before your first session.
  5. Attend all 20 hours. Arrive or log in early for every session, and stay to the end.
  6. Get your Certificate of Completion. Make copies, and send it to your court or probation officer if they need it.
  7. Apply to reinstate your license. When you're eligible, submit your certificate and pay the reinstatement fee to DDS.
