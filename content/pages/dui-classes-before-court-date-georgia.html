Yes, you can take DUI school before your court date in Georgia, and the Department of Driver Services (DDS) will count your certificate as long as you took and completed the course after the date of the violation. That's what DDS Rule 375-3-3-.05 says, and it ties the certificate to your arrest date, not your conviction date.

Whether you should finish DUI school before court is a different question. That depends on your case, and it's a legal-strategy decision for you and your attorney. This page explains what the official rules say, why some people start early, and what to ask your attorney before you decide.

What the DDS rule says about when your certificate counts

DDS Rule 375-3-3-.05, "Acceptance of Certificates for Reinstatement," is the official rule on timing. It says that to be accepted for license reinstatement, or to avoid a pending suspension, a certificate of completion from a DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program must show that the course was taken and completed after the violation date which caused the pending or active suspension.

In plain English:

  • Completed after your arrest date: the certificate meets the DDS timing rule, whether you finished before or after your court date.
  • Completed before your arrest date: DDS won't accept it for this suspension, and you'd need to take the program again.

The rule also covers Driver Improvement certificates the same way. Any exception has to be approved by the DDS Commissioner or someone the Commissioner designates.

Why the arrest date matters more than the court date for DDS

After a DUI arrest in Georgia, you can face two separate license actions. One is an administrative license suspension from DDS that can start soon after the arrest. The other is the suspension that follows a DUI conviction in court.

According to the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual, when you took the state's test and this is your first administrative suspension, reinstating it requires a Risk Reduction certificate and the reinstatement fee. The manual also says the certificate and fee you submit to reinstate the administrative suspension may be applied toward the same requirements for the suspension from a conviction for the same DUI. In other words, DDS already has a path where a certificate earned before conviction is used for the conviction suspension.

For the conviction suspension itself, a first DUI at 21 or older brings a 12-month suspension, with reinstatement possible after 120 days if you have your certificate and pay the fee. Our guide to getting your license back after a DUI walks through the full process.

Why some people choose to start DUI school early

People give a few practical reasons for finishing the program before their case is over:

  • Being ready for the reinstatement window. If you end up needing the certificate, having it done means it's not the thing holding you up when you become eligible.
  • An administrative suspension after the arrest. As described above, reinstating some administrative suspensions requires the certificate.
  • Scheduling. The course is a 20-hour class over several sessions, and schools run classes on a set schedule. DDS says strict attendance policies apply, and arriving late or missing a session can mean starting over at full cost. Some people prefer to fit it in before sentencing adds other obligations.

These are reasons people give. They aren't a recommendation that you should start early, because the right timing depends on your case.

Why you should ask your attorney before you decide

Whether to complete DUI school before court is a legal-strategy question, and we can't answer it for you. Your attorney knows the facts of your case, the court and the possible outcomes. Before you register, it's worth asking your attorney:

  • Does it make sense in my case to complete the Risk Reduction Program before my court date?
  • Will this court accept a certificate I earned before sentencing, or does it want the program completed after?
  • How and when should I give the court proof of completion?
  • If my charge is reduced or dismissed, will I still need the program for anything?

That last question matters. DDS describes the Risk Reduction Program as an intervention program for people convicted of DUI and a few other offenses. If your case ends without a conviction that requires it, you may not need the certificate for your license. Your attorney can tell you what's likely in your situation.

What the official sources don't say. DDS Rule 375-3-3-.05 tells you when DDS will accept your certificate for reinstatement. We did not find any official source that says whether a court will accept, credit or require a course you finished before your conviction. Courts set their own sentencing conditions. If you don't have an attorney, ask the court clerk how your court handles this, and ask DDS (678-413-8400) about your license record.

What about a clinical evaluation before court?

If this is your second DUI or more within 10 years, DDS also requires proof of a state-approved clinical evaluation before you can reinstate. People in that situation often ask the same timing question about the evaluation.

DDS Rule 375-3-3-.05 covers only Risk Reduction and Driver Improvement certificates. It doesn't address clinical evaluations, and clinical evaluations fall under the Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD), not DDS. So the same advice applies: ask your attorney before scheduling an evaluation while your case is open. Our page on the clinical evaluation after a second DUI explains how it works.

If you decide to start now: what to expect

If you and your attorney decide it makes sense to start, here's what the program involves, based on DDS:

  • Choose a DDS-certified program. Only certified Risk Reduction Programs count. Certificates from online DUI courses can't be used for anything related to your license.
  • Complete both parts. The program has a 130-question assessment and a 20-hour intervention course. You need both to earn your certificate.
  • Plan on about a week. DDS says the full course takes less than a week in most cases.
  • Pay the state price. Every certified program charges $360: $100 for the assessment, $235 for the course and $25 for the workbook. See how much DUI school costs in Georgia.
  • Keep your certificate safe. DDS rules say your school issues the certificate within one business day, and no more than 48 hours, after you finish. If you lose it, the school can replace it for a fee of up to $20. DDS rules only require schools to keep student records for 3 years.

Can you take DUI classes online before court?

Not a self-paced online course. DDS won't accept those certificates for your license, no matter when you take them.
