Can you take anger management classes online in Georgia?

Often you can, but the decision belongs to your court or probation officer, not the class provider. Get approval first, know the difference between live and self-paced classes, and keep your proof.

By Athena DUI Academy & Family EnrichmentLast reviewed September 28, 20267 min read

Key takeaways

  • Whether an online anger management class counts is up to your court or probation officer. Get their approval before you pay.
  • Live online classes with a real instructor at a set time are different from self-paced video courses. Ask which one your officer will accept.
  • Georgia's rule that DDS won't accept online DUI school certificates is about DUI school only. Anger management is decided case by case.
  • An order for a Family Violence Intervention Program (FVIP) can't be met with an anger management class, online or in person.
  • Keep your certificate, receipt and notes of what your probation officer approved until your probation ends.

You can often take anger management classes online in Georgia, but whether an online class counts for your case is up to your court or probation officer. Get their approval before you enroll, and ask whether they'll accept a live online class, a self-paced course, or only in-person attendance.

No class provider can promise that your court will accept its class. Only the people supervising your case can tell you that. The steps below help you get a clear answer, choose the right format, and keep the proof you'll need.

Who decides whether an online class counts

Your judge sets the conditions of your sentence, and your probation officer helps you carry them out. The Georgia Department of Community Supervision says people on supervision may be required to complete rehabilitative services as directed by their officer. That means your officer's instructions matter as much as the words in your order.

Local courts spell this out in their own paperwork. One State Court of Athens-Clarke County sentence form, for example, lets people complete certain probation requirements where they live, but says the locations must be approved by the probation officer. Your court's form may say something different, which is why asking first is so important.

You also won't find anger management classes on a Georgia state certification list. The Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) certifies programs like DUI school and Driver Improvement, and Family Violence Intervention Programs are certified by the Department of Community Supervision with help from the Georgia Commission on Family Violence. Anger management isn't part of either system, so your court or officer makes the call.

Live online vs. self-paced classes

"Online anger management" can mean two very different things. Know which one you're looking at before you ask for approval.

A live online class covers the same skills as an in-person class, such as spotting warning signs, calming down and handling conflict. We describe those in What do you learn in anger management classes? The difference is mostly where you sit.

What to ask before you enroll

Call or email your probation officer, or the clerk of the court if you aren't on supervised probation. Ask these questions and write down the answers, the date and the person's name.

  1. Will you accept an online anger management class?
  2. If so, does it have to be live with an instructor, or is self-paced OK?
  3. How many hours or sessions do I need? If your order doesn't say, this question matters most. Our guide to how long court-ordered anger management classes are explains how to read your order.
  4. Do you need to approve the provider first? If yes, ask what information they need about the class.
  5. What's my deadline?
  6. How should the certificate reach you? Directly from the provider, from you, or both.

If you can, ask for the approval in writing, such as an email or a note in your file. It protects you if your officer changes or a question comes up later.

Why DUI school's online rule doesn't answer this

You may have heard that Georgia doesn't accept online classes. That rule is about DUI school. The Georgia DDS says it can't accept certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses for anything related to your driver's license. It says nothing about anger management.

If you have a DUI too, read Can you take DUI school online in Georgia? for how that rule works. For anger management, go back to the same source every time: your order and your probation officer.

FVIP is a separate requirement, online or not

If your order says "Family Violence Intervention Program" or "FVIP," anger management will not satisfy it. Georgia requires a certified FVIP. These are 24-week programs certified by the Department of Community Supervision, with the Georgia Commission on Family Violence (GCFV) helping to train, certify and monitor them. Georgia's FVIP rules say a program may not give FVIP credit for an anger management class. Find a certified program in the directory at gcfv.georgia.gov. Athena does not offer FVIP.

This mix-up is common because both programs deal with conflict. But GCFV explains they're built on different ideas, and Georgia law generally requires an FVIP when a court sentences someone for an offense involving family violence, unless the court states on the record why it isn't appropriate. If your case involved family violence and you aren't sure which program you need, ask your probation officer or your attorney.

Tips for doing well in a live online class

  • Test your setup early. Check your camera, microphone and internet connection before the first session.
  • Find a quiet, private spot. Group members often share personal examples, and classes usually expect what's said to stay private.
  • Keep your camera on and stay engaged unless the instructor says otherwise. Treat it like being in the room.
  • Don't join while driving or at work. Your attention needs to be on the class.
  • Know the makeup policy. If your connection drops or you miss a session, find out how to make it up before your deadline.

What to keep as proof of completion

Proof matters more with online classes, because your officer never saw you walk into a classroom. Keep these until your probation ends:

  • Your certificate of completion. Check that it shows your full name, the class name, the hours completed, the completion date and the provider's contact details.
  • Your approval. The email, letter or notes showing your officer or the court approved an online class, with the date and name.
  • Your receipt and enrollment confirmation.
  • Proof the certificate was delivered. If the provider sends it, ask them to confirm when they did. Then check with your officer that it arrived.

Frequently asked questions

Do courts accept online anger management classes in Georgia?

There's no single statewide answer. Each court and probation officer decides what will satisfy your order, and some care about whether the class is live or self-paced. Ask your probation officer or the clerk before you enroll, and note who approved it and when.

What's the difference between live online and self-paced anger management?

A live online class meets at a scheduled time with an instructor and other students on video, so you take part in real time. A self-paced course is usually videos or reading you finish on your own. If your officer approves online classes, ask whether a self-paced course counts or only a live one.

Can I take a cheap online anger management course for court?

You can, but it's a risk if you haven't confirmed it will count. If your court or probation officer won't accept it, you may have to pay for and complete another class before your deadline. Checking first is the safest way to pay only once.

Can I take a Family Violence Intervention Program online instead of anger management?

An FVIP and anger management are separate requirements in Georgia. If your order says FVIP, you need a certified Family Violence Intervention Program, and an anger management class won't count toward it. Use the directory at gcfv.georgia.gov to find a certified FVIP and ask the program how it runs its classes.

How do I send my online anger management certificate to the court?

Ask your probation officer or the clerk how they want to receive it and by what date. Some class providers send it for you. At Athena, we send certificates to the court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost when needed, and you should keep your own copy either way.
