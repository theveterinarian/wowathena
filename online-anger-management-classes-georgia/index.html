Can you take anger management classes online in Georgia?

Often you can, but the decision belongs to your court or probation officer, not the class provider. Get approval first, know the difference between live, self-paced and in-person classes, and keep your proof.

By Athena DUI Academy & Family EnrichmentLast reviewed September 28, 202614 min read

Key takeaways

  • Whether an online anger management class counts is up to your court or probation officer. Get their approval before you pay.
  • Live online classes with a real instructor at a set time are different from self-paced video courses. Ask which one your officer will accept.
  • Georgia's rule that DDS won't accept online DUI school certificates is about self-paced DUI courses. Certified DUI schools may teach live online, and anger management is decided case by case.
  • An order for a Family Violence Intervention Program (FVIP) can't be met with an anger management class, online or in person. Certified FVIPs may run online classes under Georgia's rule, but only a certified program counts.
  • Keep your certificate, receipt, approval and notes of what your probation officer approved until your probation ends.

You can often take anger management classes online in Georgia, but whether an online class counts for your case is up to your court or probation officer. Get their approval before you enroll, and ask whether they'll accept a live online class, a self-paced course, or only in-person attendance.

No class provider can promise that your court will accept its class. Only the people supervising your case can tell you that. The steps below help you get a clear answer, choose the right format, and keep the proof you'll need.

Who decides whether an online class counts

Your judge sets the conditions of your sentence, and your probation officer helps you carry them out. The Georgia Department of Community Supervision (DCS) says a court may impose any special conditions it finds necessary and appropriate for the case, and that people on supervision may be required to complete rehabilitative services as directed by their officer. That means your officer's instructions matter as much as the words in your order.

Local courts spell this out in their own paperwork. The State Court of Athens-Clarke County sentence form, for example, lets people complete certain probation requirements where they live, but says the locations must be approved by the probation officer. Your court's form may say something different, which is why asking first is so important.

You also won't find anger management classes on a Georgia state certification list. The Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) certifies programs like DUI school and Driver Improvement, and Family Violence Intervention Programs are certified by DCS with help from the Georgia Commission on Family Violence. Anger management isn't part of either system, so your court or officer makes the call.

"Online anger management" can mean two very different things. Know which one you're looking at before you ask for approval, and understand how each compares with sitting in a classroom.

Checklist: get approval before you enroll

Work through these steps in order. Call or email your probation officer, or the clerk of the court if you aren't on supervised probation. Write down the answers, the date and the person's name.

  1. Read your order first. Note the hours or sessions, any named provider, the deadline, and whether it says "anger management" or "FVIP." Our guide to how long court-ordered anger management classes are explains how to read it.
  2. Pick a specific class to ask about. Officers can answer "is this class OK?" more easily than "are online classes OK?" Have the provider's name, the format, the hours, the schedule and the finish date ready.
  3. Ask: "Will you accept an online anger management class?"
  4. Ask: "Does it have to be live with an instructor, or is self-paced OK?"
  5. Ask: "How many hours or sessions do I need?" If your order doesn't say, this question matters most.
  6. Ask: "Do you need to approve the provider first?" If yes, ask what information they need about the class.
  7. Ask: "What's my deadline, and which date does it run from?"
  8. Ask: "How should the certificate reach you?" Directly from the provider, from you, or both.
  9. Get it in writing. An email or a note in your file protects you if your officer changes or a question comes up later.
  10. Only then register and pay. If anything about the class changes, such as the schedule or format, tell your officer.

Why DUI school's online rule doesn't answer this

You may have heard that Georgia doesn't accept online classes. That rule is about DUI school. The Georgia DDS says it can't accept certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses for anything related to your driver's license. It says nothing about anger management.

Even within DUI school, Georgia draws the same line this page draws. DDS Rule 375-5-6-.18 lets a certified Risk Reduction Program teach through a "remote classroom" over services such as Zoom or Webex, as long as the instruction is synchronous: real-time teaching and interaction, on a specific platform, at a specific time. Schools must notify DDS at least 24 hours before such a class. DDS's own definitions describe synchronous instruction as instructors and students interacting on a specific online platform at a specific time. In other words, the state itself treats a live online class and a self-paced course as different things.

If you have a DUI too, read Can you take DUI school online in Georgia? for how that rule works. For anger management, go back to the same source every time: your order and your probation officer.

FVIP is a separate requirement, online or not

If your order says "Family Violence Intervention Program" or "FVIP," anger management will not satisfy it. Georgia requires a certified FVIP. These are 24-week programs certified by DCS, with the Georgia Commission on Family Violence (GCFV) helping to train, certify and monitor them. Georgia's Rule 105-3-.07 says an FVIP may not give FVIP credit for an anger management, DUI or any other class. Find a certified program in the directory at gcfv.georgia.gov. Athena does not offer FVIP.

Online FVIP classes do exist, but only from certified programs, and only under strict rules. Rule 105-3-.07 says participants may attend FVIP classes in person or online. Online classes must run on an approved platform (the rule lists Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, GoTo Meeting, Skype, Cisco Webex and several others), participants must keep their video on the whole time, and no one else may be in the room. The class must still deliver at least 90 minutes of instruction, and the program still requires 24 weekly classes with no more than one class per week. GCFV says people ordered to FVIP may attend any certified program on its list, not just one in their own judicial circuit.

This mix-up is common because both programs deal with conflict. But GCFV explains they're built on different ideas, and it quotes Georgia law requiring a court to order an FVIP when it sentences someone for an offense involving family violence, unless the court states on the record why that isn't appropriate. If your case involved family violence and you aren't sure which program you need, ask your probation officer or your attorney.

Tips for doing well in a live online class

  • Test your setup early. Check your camera, microphone and internet connection before the first session.
  • Find a quiet, private spot. Group members often share personal examples, and classes usually expect what's said to stay private.
  • Keep your camera on and stay engaged unless the instructor says otherwise. Treat it like being in the room.
  • Don't join while driving or at work. Your attention needs to be on the class.
  • Know the makeup policy. If your connection drops or you miss a session, find out how to make it up before your deadline.

What happens if...

Your officer says no after you already paid

Then the class doesn't count for your order, no matter how good it was. You'll need to complete a class the officer will accept, likely at your own expense, and still meet your deadline. Whether the first provider refunds you depends on its policy. This is the main reason to ask before you pay.

Your connection drops mid-session

Reconnect as quickly as you can and tell the instructor what happened. Whether the session still counts depends on the provider's attendance policy, so know it in advance. If it doesn't count, arrange a makeup immediately and tell your officer the new finish date.

You move during probation

The Athens-Clarke County form lets some probation requirements be completed where you live, but only at locations your officer approves, and the same form requires you to get permission before moving or leaving the court's jurisdiction. A live online class may make this easier, but the approval still has to come from your officer, not from you.

The provider can't verify your attendance

An officer who has doubts about a self-paced course may ask the provider to confirm you did the work. If the provider can't, you may be asked to take another class. Choosing a class with a real instructor and a real roster avoids this.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming "online" means one thing. A live class and a self-paced course are different, and your officer may accept one but not the other.
  • Paying before asking. If the answer turns out to be no, you pay twice.
  • Applying the DUI school rule to anger management. The DDS online rule covers DUI school certificates only.
  • Taking anger management for an FVIP order. Georgia's rule says an FVIP can't give credit for it, online or in person.
  • Losing the approval. If your officer changes, the new officer may not know what the old one agreed to. Keep the email.
  • Not confirming delivery. A certificate that was "sent" but never arrived doesn't help you. Check.

Key terms

  • Live online class. A class held on a video platform at a set time, with an instructor and other students taking part in real time.
  • Synchronous instruction. DDS's term for the same idea: instructors and students interacting on a specific online platform at a specific time.
  • Self-paced course. Videos, reading or quizzes you complete on your own schedule, usually without a live instructor.
  • Probation officer. The person supervising your probation, who under DCS's standard conditions can direct you to rehabilitative services.
  • Special condition. A condition a court adds because of the nature of the case, according to DCS. Anger management is one example.
  • Certificate of completion. The provider's proof that you finished. For anger management, no Georgia rule sets its contents, so check it yourself.
  • Family Violence Intervention Program (FVIP). Georgia's 24-week certified program for family violence offenses. Not the same as anger management.
  • Approval. Your officer's or court's confirmation that a specific class will satisfy your order. Get it in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Do courts accept online anger management classes in Georgia?

There's no single statewide answer. Each court and probation officer decides what will satisfy your order, and some care about whether the class is live or self-paced. Ask your probation officer or the clerk before you enroll, and note who approved it and when.

What's the difference between live online and self-paced anger management?

A live online class meets at a scheduled time with an instructor and other students on video, so you take part in real time. A self-paced course is usually videos or reading you finish on your own. If your officer approves online classes, ask whether a self-paced course counts or only a live one.

Can I take a cheap online anger management course for court?

You can, but it's a risk if you haven't confirmed it will count. If your court or probation officer won't accept it, you may have to pay for and complete another class before your deadline. Checking first is the safest way to pay only once.

How do I get my probation officer to approve an online anger management class?

Call or email before you enroll and give the specifics: the provider's name, whether the class is live or self-paced, the total hours, the schedule and what the certificate will show. Ask for a yes or no in writing, or ask the officer to note the approval in your file. Keep that email or note with your certificate.

Can I take a Family Violence Intervention Program online instead of anger management?

An FVIP and anger management are separate requirements in Georgia. If your order says FVIP, you need a certified Family Violence Intervention Program, and an anger management class won't count toward it. Georgia's FVIP rule does allow certified programs to hold classes online on approved video platforms with cameras on, so use the directory at gcfv.georgia.gov and ask the program how it runs its classes.

Does the Georgia DDS rule against online classes apply to anger management?

No. That rule is about DUI school: DDS says it can't accept certificates from online DUI, alcohol or drug use risk reduction courses for license purposes. Anger management isn't a DDS program, so your court or probation officer decides whether an online class counts for you.

What if my internet drops during a live online anger management class?

Reconnect as fast as you can and message the instructor so they know what happened. Whether the session still counts is up to the provider's attendance policy, so ask about it before your first class. If you end up missing a session, find out how to make it up and keep your court deadline in mind.

What proof do I need from an online anger management class?

A certificate of completion with your full name, the class name, the hours completed, the completion date and the provider's contact details. Keep your receipt, your enrollment confirmation and the email or note showing your officer approved an online class. Then confirm the certificate actually arrived where it was supposed to go.

How do I send my online anger management certificate to the court?

Ask your probation officer or the clerk how they want to receive it and by what date. Some class providers send it for you. At Athena, we send certificates to the court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost when needed, and you should keep your own copy either way.
