What family enrichment classes are

Family enrichment classes teach the skills that make a home run better: how to talk so the other person actually hears you, how to listen without jumping to the fix, how parents set limits and keep them, and how to settle disagreements before they turn into fights. The "Family Enrichment" in our name is this side of what we do. Most people who sit in our DUI, Driver Improvement and anger management classes go home to a family, and the skills in this class are the ones they tell us they wish they had learned sooner.

You do not need to be in any kind of trouble to take one. Many students enroll for themselves, as new parents, as co-parents after a separation, or as couples who keep having the same argument. Others come because a judge, probation officer, caseworker or attorney asked them to complete a parenting or family-focused class.

Who the classes are for

  • Parents and step-parents who want calmer, more consistent discipline and better day-to-day communication with their children.
  • Co-parents who are separated or divorced and need to raise children across two homes without putting the children in the middle.
  • Couples who want tools for conflict, money stress, trust and the everyday friction of life together.
  • Grandparents and relatives raising children.
  • People with a court, probation or agency request for a parenting or family class. See the section below before you enroll.

What you learn

Classes are practical and discussion based, with short exercises you can use at home that week. Topics include:

  • Communication: speaking without blame, listening to understand, and repairing a conversation after it goes wrong.
  • Parenting skills: age-appropriate expectations, routines, consistent consequences, praise that works, and staying calm when a child pushes back.
  • Conflict resolution: noticing the early signs of an argument, taking a time-out that does not feel like walking away, and coming back to solve the actual problem.
  • Stress and emotions: how stress, anger and worry show up at home, and how to manage them before they land on the people you love.
  • Healthy relationships and boundaries: respect, trust, personal responsibility and knowing where one person's job ends and another's begins.
  • Co-parenting: keeping adult disagreements away from children, consistent rules across two homes, and handovers that stay civil.

If a court, probation officer or agency sent you

Read your paperwork before you enroll, because the wording decides what counts.

  • "Parenting class" or "family class" with no program named. A family enrichment class is usually what is meant. Confirm with the court, your probation officer or your caseworker that our class is acceptable, and ask what proof they need.
  • A set number of hours or weeks. Tell us the number when you call so we put you in a class that meets it.
  • "Family Violence Intervention Program" or "FVIP". This is a specific 24-week program certified by the Georgia Department of Community Supervision with the Georgia Commission on Family Violence. A family enrichment class or an anger management class does not satisfy an FVIP order. If that is what your order says, you need a certified FVIP provider.
  • A deadline. Classes start on scheduled dates and you cannot register and start the same day, so call as soon as you have the order.

We will tell you straight. Bring or read us your order when you call. If our class does not fit what it asks for, we will say so rather than let you spend money on the wrong thing.

Frequently asked questions

What is a family enrichment class?

A family enrichment class teaches practical skills for life at home: how family members talk and listen to each other, how parents set limits and stay consistent, how to handle conflict without it escalating, and how to keep relationships healthy under stress. People take them to improve their own family life or because a court, probation officer or agency asked them to.

Who are family enrichment classes for?

Parents, co-parents, couples, grandparents raising children and anyone who wants better relationships at home. You do not need a court order to enroll. Call 706.215.9661 and we will match you to the right class.

Will a family enrichment class satisfy my court order?

That depends on what the order says. If it asks for a parenting or family class without naming a specific program, a family enrichment class is often what is meant, but only the court or your probation officer can confirm that. If it names a Family Violence Intervention Program, you need that specific certified program instead. Bring your paperwork and we will tell you honestly whether our class fits.

Is this the same as a Family Violence Intervention Program?

No. A Family Violence Intervention Program, or FVIP, is a 24-week program certified by the Georgia Department of Community Supervision with the Georgia Commission on Family Violence, ordered in family violence cases. Family enrichment classes are shorter skills classes and do not take the place of an FVIP.

Can I take family enrichment classes online?

Yes. Athena teaches family enrichment classes live online as well as in person at the Athens office. If a court or agency sent you, check with them first about whether a live online class is accepted.

How do I register and what does it cost?

Register online using the Register button, or call 706.215.9661. Pricing is not published online, so call for the current price and the next available class. You join the next scheduled class rather than starting the same day you register.

Do you send proof of completion?

Yes, when it is needed. Athena sends completion certificates to your court, probation officer, caseworker or clerk at no extra cost. Keep a copy for your own records.
