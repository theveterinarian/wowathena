A DUI clinical evaluation is a face-to-face interview with a state-approved evaluator that looks at your alcohol and drug use and decides whether you need treatment. In Georgia you need one to reinstate your license after two or more DUI convictions within 10 years, or whenever a court orders it. Athena DUI Academy offers DUI clinical evaluations in Athens for $150.

If you've just been told you need an evaluation, this page explains what it is, what happens in the room, what the rules require afterward and how it fits with DUI school and your license.

What is a DUI clinical evaluation?

Georgia's rules define a clinical evaluation as the process used to diagnose a person's substance abuse or dependence and, if needed, refer them to the right treatment. It's run under the Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD), not DDS. DBHDD keeps a Registry of Clinical Evaluators and a Registry of Treatment Providers, and an evaluation only counts if it's done by an evaluator on the registry at a DBHDD-approved location.

The evaluation process, step by step

Georgia's DBHDD rules (Chapter 82-2-1) spell out each step. Here they are in order.

  1. Finish DUI school first if you can. The rules require the evaluator to review the standardized screening you took at the Risk Reduction Program, and DDS policy requires the school to send your assessment results to your evaluator within five business days of the request. If you still need DUI school, see DUI school in Athens.
  2. Choose an evaluator on DBHDD's registry. Evaluations can only be given at DBHDD-approved locations by registered evaluators.
  3. Sign a written contract. Rule 82-2-1-.09 requires a DBHDD-approved contract, with a copy given to you before any service.
  4. The clinical interview. A face-to-face conversation that gathers your background, medical history, the alcohol concentration from your current offense, social and family history, substance use history, work background and mental status.
  5. Assessment tools. The evaluator uses one or more DBHDD-approved assessment instruments, plus your DUI school screening.
  6. The written report, within 7 days. The evaluator must complete a report within seven days of the interview. It must contain enough information to diagnose or rule out a substance-related disorder under current DSM criteria and to recommend an ASAM level of service, and it must show the referral and the basis for it.
  7. One of two outcomes. Either a referral to treatment, with a list of approved providers for that level, or a finding that no treatment is needed, which goes to DBHDD for review.
  8. Your certificate. A Requirements Met Certificate from DBHDD if no treatment was needed, or a Certificate of Treatment Completion from your provider once treatment is done.
  9. Reinstatement. Take the certificate, your Risk Reduction certificate and the fee to DDS, or mail them to the DDS Validation Unit, P.O. Box 80447, Conyers, Georgia 30013. Our reinstatement guide covers the DDS side.

If no treatment is recommended

For a first conviction, the evaluator sends DBHDD a summary within seven days of the interview. For a multiple or habitual offender, the evaluator sends the complete evaluation. The rules say DBHDD will finish its review within two weeks of receiving it. If it approves, DBHDD issues a Requirements Met Certificate directly to you. If it doesn't agree, it asks the evaluator to add a referral to a specific ASAM level; if the evaluator disagrees, a panel of three professional peers decides and both sides abide by it.

If treatment is recommended

The evaluator recommends either short-term treatment, for clients needing services no higher than ASAM level I, or longer-term treatment, for clients needing level I or higher, and gives you a list of DBHDD-approved providers for that level. DBHDD sets the range of hours per week and weeks by policy. The treatment rules then apply:

  • You must stay in treatment at least as long as the evaluator recommended, and complete at least the days and hours per week recommended. The provider may require more.
  • Longer-term treatment is at least three hours of treatment per week. Twelve-step or self-help meetings may be required but don't count toward the three hours.
  • A client who has complied with the treatment plan can't be required to stay longer than one year.
  • Providers may only enroll clients whose referral matches the ASAM level they offer, unless there is no matching provider in your area and DBHDD approves a different one.
  • If more than 60 days pass between the evaluation report and enrollment, the provider may re-evaluate you to confirm the level and hours.
  • Treatment only happens at DBHDD-approved locations, and providers may only collect fees DBHDD has authorized.

How it fits with DUI school and the interlock permit

For a second DUI in five years, DDS says the license is suspended at least 18 months with no driving at all for the first 120 days. After that hard suspension, DDS Rule 375-3-3-.10 lets you apply for an ignition interlock limited permit if you provide proof of an installed, approved interlock device, a Certificate of Completion from a Risk Reduction Program, and court authorization showing you're enrolled in a drug court or clinical treatment that satisfies O.C.G.A. § 40-5-63.1. The interlock must stay on for 12 months, and DDS's chart notes that if you also refused the state's test, the interlock permit is delayed until that implied consent suspension is served.

So the order most people follow is: DUI school, then the clinical evaluation, then enrollment in any recommended treatment, then the permit application. The evaluation report is what documents the treatment level the court and DDS rely on. Our ignition interlock guide covers the device, and this page covers limited permits generally.

What to bring

The interview covers set topics, so having these on hand makes it go faster and keeps the report accurate.

  • Your court paperwork or probation conditions, so the evaluator knows why you were referred.
  • The alcohol concentration from your current offense, if you have it. The rules list it as part of the interview.
  • Your Risk Reduction Certificate of Completion, or the name of the school so we can request your assessment results.
  • Your medical history, current medications and any prior treatment, because the interview gathers medical and substance use history.
  • Your DDS record printout from Online Services, which shows prior convictions and your reinstatement steps.

What happens if…

You skip the recommended treatment

No Certificate of Treatment Completion can be issued before treatment is complete, and without it DDS won't have the proof it requires for two DUIs in ten years. Only DBHDD, the evaluator, the provider or an accountability court's treatment team may direct the clinical side of your treatment.

You wait more than 60 days to enroll

The provider may re-evaluate you using the report to confirm the level of service, days and hours.

You use a provider that isn't on the registry

Treatment only counts at DBHDD-approved locations with approved providers, and the provider must offer the ASAM level you were referred to. Check DBHDD's DUI Intervention Program site before you enroll.

You disagree with the evaluation

The rules give the peer review process to DBHDD and the evaluator, not the client. If you believe the evaluation was wrong, talk to your attorney about your options.

Special situations

Third DUI in five years

DDS declares you a habitual violator with a five-year revocation, and you're subject to the 12-month interlock requirement when you become eligible. DDS's chart says a habitual violator probationary license with interlock may be available after two years with court permission. The evaluation and any treatment are still required.

Accountability court

Athens-Clarke County's State Court runs a DUI/Drug Court that provides supervision, treatment and counseling. If you're in a program like this, the rules let the court's treatment team direct your treatment, so follow their instructions on evaluators and providers.

Court-ordered on a first DUI

If your sentence requires an evaluation, the court's deadline controls even though DDS doesn't need it to reinstate. Ask your probation officer who should receive the report or certificate.

Key terms

  • Clinical evaluation: DBHDD's process to diagnose substance abuse or dependence and refer to treatment if indicated.
  • Clinical interview: the face-to-face part of the evaluation covering history, the offense BAC and mental status.
  • ASAM level of service: the American Society of Addiction Medicine placement criteria the evaluator uses to recommend a treatment level.
  • Requirements Met Certificate: the DBHDD document issued when an evaluation finding no treatment is approved.
  • Certificate of Treatment Completion: the pre-numbered state document a provider issues when treatment is finished.
  • Multiple or habitual offender: under the DBHDD rules, a person with two or more DUI convictions.
  • Risk Reduction Program: DUI school; the evaluator reviews its screening.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a DUI clinical evaluation in Georgia?

At Athena DUI Academy in Athens, a DUI clinical evaluation is $150. Any treatment the evaluator recommends is a separate cost paid to the treatment provider, and Georgia's rules only let providers collect fees that DBHDD has authorized.

Do I need a clinical evaluation for a first DUI in Georgia?

DDS's DUI chart says a clinical evaluation is not required by DDS to reinstate a first DUI within five years, but courts may make it a condition of probation. DBHDD's own page says DUI offenders are required to have one. Read your sentence, and ask your attorney or probation officer if it isn't clear.

What questions do they ask at a DUI evaluation?

Georgia's rules say the interview gathers your background, medical history, the alcohol concentration from your current offense, social and family history, substance use history, work background and mental status. The evaluator also reviews the screening you took in DUI school and uses at least one DBHDD-approved assessment tool.

How long does a DUI clinical evaluation take to come back?

The evaluator must complete the written report within seven days of your interview. If no treatment is recommended, the evaluation goes to DBHDD, which the rules say will finish its review within two weeks of receiving it and then issue a Requirements Met Certificate to you.

What if the evaluator says I need treatment?

You get a list of DBHDD-approved providers for the level recommended, either short-term or longer-term. You must stay at least as long as recommended; longer-term treatment is at least three hours a week, and a client who follows the plan can't be kept longer than one year. The provider issues a Certificate of Treatment Completion when you finish.

Can I get my license back before I finish treatment?

The Certificate of Treatment Completion can't be issued before treatment is complete, and the rules say it may be withheld until treatment fees are paid. For a second DUI in five years, an ignition interlock limited permit is possible after 120 days if you're enrolled in qualifying treatment or a drug court and the court authorizes it.

Should I do DUI school before the clinical evaluation?

It helps. Georgia's rules require the evaluator to review the standardized screening you took at the Risk Reduction Program, and DDS policy requires the school to send a copy of your assessment results to your evaluator within five business days of the request. Athena offers both, so you can schedule them in order.

Is a clinical evaluation the same as DUI school?

No. DUI school, the Risk Reduction Program, is a group course with a 130-question assessment and 20 hours of class. A clinical evaluation is a separate one-on-one interview with a licensed or certified evaluator. Many people with repeat DUIs need both.

What if I lose my treatment completion certificate?

The treatment provider can issue a replacement, and Georgia's rules cap the cost at $15. Certificates are official state documents, so altering or falsifying one is a crime.

Who approves DUI evaluators in Georgia?

The Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD) keeps a Registry of Clinical Evaluators and a Registry of Treatment Providers. Evaluations only count if done by an evaluator on the registry at a DBHDD-approved location. DBHDD's DUI Intervention Program site lets you search approved providers.
