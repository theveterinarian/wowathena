How long is DUI school in Georgia?

DUI school in Georgia is 20 hours of class plus a short assessment, and most people finish within a week. Here's how the time is split up, every deadline that applies, what slows people down, and how the class lines up with getting your license back.

By Athena DUI Academy & Family EnrichmentLast reviewed September 28, 202615 min read

Key takeaways

  • Georgia DUI school is a 20-hour class (the Intervention Component) plus a 130-question assessment taken first.
  • DDS says the whole course takes less than a week in most cases.
  • State rules require every session to be completed within four weeks, with no more than two sessions in one day and a meal break of at least an hour between them.
  • Your assessment is valid for one year. If you don't enroll in the class within that year, you'll need a new assessment and a new fee.
  • For a first DUI at 21 or older, you can apply to reinstate after 120 days of a 12-month suspension if you have your Certificate of Completion and pay the fee. A second DUI in five years brings a 120-day hard suspension and then 12 months on an interlock.
  • The delays that cost people the most: arriving late, missing a session, a class cancelled for fewer than five students, and letting the assessment expire.

DUI school in Georgia is a 20-hour class plus a 130-question assessment, and most people finish the whole thing in less than a week. The 20 hours are split into several sessions, and state rules say all of them must be completed within four weeks, with no more than two sessions on any one day.

The official name is the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program. The hours are the same at every certified school in Georgia, and the same for live online and in-person classes. What changes from school to school is how those hours are laid out across the week. This page covers the hours, the session rules, every deadline that applies, what slows people down, and how the class fits the reinstatement and interlock clocks.

How many hours of DUI school are required?

DUI school has two parts, and you need both to earn your Certificate of Completion.

PartWhat it isTime
Assessment ComponentA 130-question screening about your alcohol and drug use and how it affects your drivingDone before the class starts; processed at least 30 minutes before the first session
Intervention ComponentThe class: therapeutic education and peer group counseling in a group setting20 hours, over several sessions
Meal breaksRequired when two sessions are held on the same dayAt least 1 hour, on top of the 20 hours

Georgia's rules require every student to attend the class "for the entire 20 hours," and no one may be admitted late or dismissed early from any session. There's no shortcut. Breaks within a session follow the course syllabus, and DDS policy says the meal break between two same-day sessions is in addition to the 20 hours, not part of them.

DUI school session by session

The rules don't fix how many sessions a school runs or how long each one is. They fix the frame: 20 hours in total, sessions in order, at most two a day, everything inside four weeks, following the schedule the school has on file with DDS. Within that frame, here's what happens at each stage.

Ask the school for its full session schedule before you sign anything. Rule 375-5-6-.15 requires classes to follow the schedule on file with DDS, and any changes have to be submitted to DDS before the class begins, so the dates you're given should be the dates you get.

The four-week rule and the two-sessions-a-day rule

DDS says that "in most cases, the entire 20-hour course roughly takes less than a week." The exact number of days depends on the school's schedule, because the rules allow up to two sessions a day. A school may spread the sessions across more days, or hold two in one day with a meal break between them.

The rules that shape every schedule:

  • No more than two sessions in a single day. Together with the 20-hour total, that's why DDS describes the class as several days of lessons.
  • All sessions within four weeks. Programs must schedule the class so every session is completed inside that window.
  • Sessions in order. DDS policy says you must attend all sessions in sequence, and you can't go on to the next session after missing one.
  • Same class, start to finish. Students from one class can't be mixed into another to finish sessions, except to make up an excused absence.
  • Five to 40 students. A class needs at least five paid contracts and can't exceed 40 students or the school's certified maximum.

Before you sign up, look at every session date and time, not only the first one. DDS advises taking any scheduling conflicts into account before you sign a contract to attend.

How long do you have to finish DUI school?

Three timelines can apply, and it helps to keep them separate.

  1. Your court's deadline. If a judge ordered you to complete DUI school, your sentencing paperwork may say by when. That deadline comes from your court, not from DDS or the school. If you can't tell what your order requires, ask your attorney or the court.
  2. Your assessment's shelf life. The assessment is valid for one year. DDS policy says schools must tell you that, and that if you don't enroll in the class within that year, you'll need another assessment and another assessment fee. A new conviction in that year, or later, also requires a new assessment.
  3. The four-week class window. Once your class starts, the school must schedule all sessions within four weeks.

Your license is the other clock. It stays suspended until you meet DDS's reinstatement requirements, and after a DUI your Certificate of Completion is one of them. Waiting to take the class usually means waiting longer to drive legally. One more timing rule: under Rule 375-3-3-.05, DDS only accepts a certificate for a course taken and completed after the violation date that caused your suspension, so a class from an earlier case won't count for this one.

How DUI school fits the 120-day reinstatement for a first DUI

For a first DUI in five years at age 21 or older, DDS suspends your license for 12 months. After 120 days, you can apply for reinstatement if you meet the conditions, which include presenting a Certificate of Completion from a DDS-approved Risk Reduction Program and paying the reinstatement fee.

StepTiming

Reinstatement fee paid to DDS$200 by mail, $210 in person

Because the class itself only takes about a week, the thing most likely to hold you up at day 120 is not having started. Having your certificate ready before you reach that date means you can apply as soon as you're eligible. If you're thinking about starting before your case is resolved, read whether you can start DUI classes before your court date and check with your attorney.

If you refused the state's chemical test, the rules are different: DDS says a refusal brings a suspension of at least one year and makes you ineligible for a limited driving permit if you're convicted of a first DUI. How DUI school fits the interlock and clinical evaluation periods

A second DUI changes the clocks around the class, not the class itself. The DDS Driver's Manual says a second DUI conviction within five years brings a suspension of at least 18 months. For the first 120 days you have no driving privileges at all, which the manual calls the "hard suspension." After that, you may be eligible to drive with a certified ignition interlock device installed for 12 months.

The 20-hour class fits inside that first 120 days if you want it to, and a live online class means you don't need a ride to get there. Two or more DUI convictions within 10 years also require documented proof of a state-approved clinical evaluation before DDS will reinstate, and the evaluation may recommend treatment. Plan for that on top of DUI school, and give it time: DDS policy says your school must send your assessment results to the evaluator you choose within five business days of your request. We explain the process in Second DUI in Georgia: the clinical evaluation and Ignition interlock in Georgia.

Excused absences and makeups

The rules make one exception to the "same class, start to finish" requirement. If your absence is excused, you may attend another class to make up the sessions you missed. An absence counts as excused if:

  • you're called for military duty;
  • you have a personal medical emergency, or a medical emergency involving an immediate family member; or
  • there's a death in your immediate family.

The school owner may excuse other absences at their discretion, but doesn't have to. Everything else, including refunds, is governed by the contract you signed. DDS says the decision to issue a refund or make up a missed portion of the course is a matter between you and the school.

What delays people

Arriving late or missing a session

This is where most delays come from. DDS says students who arrive late to any session "can expect to be denied entry," and students who are absent or leave early may need to start the course over again at full cost. Because sessions run in sequence, missing one means you can't take the next, and without an excused absence you can't slot into another class to catch up.

A class cancelled for fewer than five students

Georgia's rules require a minimum of five paid contracts for a scheduled class, and if fewer than five students show up for the first session, the owner may teach, cancel or reschedule. DDS explains that the class depends on group interaction, which is hard with fewer than five people. If your class is cancelled, DDS allows your assessment results to be transferred to another school, and DDS policy says no transfer fee can be charged for a cancellation. The rules also note that a student can only be counted toward the five paid contracts three times while an assessment is valid, so repeated cancellations can eventually mean a new assessment.

Letting the assessment expire

If more than a year passes between your assessment and enrolling in the class, you'll pay for a new assessment before you can start.

Moving, or a school moving or closing

If you move at least 30 miles from your school, DDS allows your assessment to be transferred, for a fee of up to $25, after verbal approval from DDS. If your school relocates 10 or more miles, or announces it's closing, the rules let students with a valid assessment who haven't finished the class cancel for a full refund or transfer to another program at no charge.

Waiting on the court

Some people wait for sentencing before enrolling, some enroll right away. Which is right for you depends on your case and your attorney's advice. What's certain is that the class must be taken after the violation date, and the assessment is good for a year, so there's room to plan.

Live online classes count the same way. In a live virtual class you must be logged in on time, on camera for every session and stay until the end. A dropped connection or a camera switched off can cost you a session. See how live online DUI classes work in Georgia.

How to plan DUI school around work

  • Get the whole schedule first. Every session date and time, before you sign. DDS says to weigh scheduling conflicts before you sign a contract.
  • Block the full session, plus travel. No one may be admitted late or dismissed early, so a session that overlaps a shift by 15 minutes is a session you'll miss.
  • Ask how the 20 hours are split. Two sessions in one day with a meal break can mean fewer days off work; one session a day can mean shorter blocks. Schools set this within the rules.
  • Consider a live virtual class. It removes the drive, and DDS accepts it from a certified program.
  • Sort out transportation. If your license is suspended, don't drive to class without a permit that allows it. DDS says a limited driving permit can cover attending a driver education program or an alcohol or drug assessment and treatment program, along with work, school, medical care and court. See Georgia limited driving permit after a DUI.
  • Tell your employer only what you need to. Your assessment results and school records are confidential under Georgia's rules and can't be released without your written consent, a court order or another legal requirement.

How long until you get your certificate?

Georgia's rules require the school to issue your Certificate of Completion "within one business day, but no more than 48 hours," after you successfully complete all program requirements. Before it's issued, your instructor gives a post-test and confirms you've met every course requirement. The rules also forbid a school from withholding a certificate from a student who has completed all requirements because of unpaid fees.

Keep the certificate somewhere safe. If you lose it, the school that taught your class can issue a replacement within five business days for a fee of up to $20. Schools only have to keep student records for three years, so don't count on getting a copy many years later. For what the sessions themselves are like, see what you learn in DUI school.

Key terms

  • Intervention Component: The 20-hour class: therapeutic education about alcohol, drug use and driving plus peer group counseling, using a DDS-approved curriculum.
  • Assessment Component: The 130-question NEEDS Assessment, taken before the class and valid for one year.
  • Session: A segment of the Intervention Component. No more than two per day, all within four weeks, always in order.
  • Excused absence: Military duty, a personal or immediate-family medical emergency, or a death in your immediate family. The only absences the rules require a school to let you make up in another class.
  • Certificate of Completion: The document issued within one business day of finishing both components. DDS needs it to reinstate your license.
  • Hard suspension: The first 120 days of a second-DUI suspension, when no driving is allowed at all.
  • Limited driving permit: A restricted permit that can allow driving to work, school, medical care, court and a driver education or alcohol/drug assessment and treatment program. DDS decides eligibility.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours is DUI school in Georgia?

The class portion is 20 hours, and you must attend all of it. You also take a 130-question assessment before the class starts. When two sessions fall on the same day, there's a meal break of at least an hour that doesn't count toward the 20 hours.

Can you finish DUI school in 2 days in Georgia?

Don't plan on it. The 20 hours are split into several sessions, state rules allow no more than two sessions in a single day, and DDS describes the class as several days of lessons. Most people finish in less than a week.

How long do I have to complete DUI school after a DUI in Georgia?

It depends on your case. Your court order may set a deadline, and your license stays suspended until you meet DDS's reinstatement requirements, so check your paperwork and ask your attorney, the court or DDS if you're unsure. Once you start, the school must schedule all sessions within four weeks.

How long is the DUI assessment good for in Georgia?

One year. DDS policy says schools must tell you the assessment is valid for one year, and if you don't enroll in the class within that year you'll need another assessment and another assessment fee. A new conviction also requires a new assessment.

How soon do you get your DUI school certificate in Georgia?

Georgia rules require the school to issue your Certificate of Completion within one business day, and no more than 48 hours, after you finish all program requirements. If you lose it later, the school can issue a replacement for a fee of up to $20.

What happens if I miss a DUI school session?

You may have to start the course over and pay the full cost again, because students can't be admitted late or leave early. If your absence was excused, such as military duty, a medical emergency or a death in your immediate family, the rules let you make up the missed session in another class.

Can my DUI class get cancelled?

Yes. Georgia's rules require at least five paid contracts for a scheduled class, and if fewer than five students show up for the first session, the school may teach, cancel or reschedule. If your class is cancelled, DDS allows your assessment to be transferred to another school at no charge.

Can I drive to DUI school on a suspended license in Georgia?

Only with a valid limited driving permit that allows it. DDS says a limited permit can allow driving to attend a driver education program or an alcohol or drug assessment and treatment program, along with work, school, medical care and court. If you don't have a permit, arrange a ride or ask about a live online class.

Does DUI school take longer for a second DUI?

The class is the same 20 hours, but the process around it is longer. Two or more DUIs within 10 years require a clinical evaluation before reinstatement, and a second DUI within five years brings a suspension of at least 18 months with a 120-day period of no driving followed by 12 months on an ignition interlock.

Does an online DUI class take less time?

A live virtual class from a certified school is the same 20 hours, scheduled the same way, with the same attendance rules. Self-paced online courses may be shorter, but DDS doesn't accept their certificates, so they add time rather than saving it.
