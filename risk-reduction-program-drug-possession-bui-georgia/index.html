Not just DUI: drug possession, underage alcohol and BUI can also require Georgia's Risk Reduction Program

Georgia DDS says DUI school is also for people convicted of drug possession, underage possession of alcohol while driving, and Boating Under the Influence. Here's when you need it, what happens to your license or boating privilege in each case, what changed in 2015, and how the four compare.

By Athena DUI Academy & Family EnrichmentLast reviewed September 28, 202617 min read

Key takeaways

  • Georgia DDS says the Risk Reduction Program is for people convicted of DUI, possession of illegal drugs, underage possession of alcohol while driving, or Boating Under the Influence.
  • Since a 2015 law change, a drug possession offense committed on or after July 1, 2015 no longer automatically suspends your Georgia license, unless it's a felony in which a motor vehicle was used.
  • DUI drugs is different: DDS lists a 180-day suspension for a first conviction in 5 years, and you need a DUI school certificate and a $210 fee ($200 online or by mail) to reinstate.
  • A court may order anyone convicted of an underage alcohol offense to finish DUI school within 120 days. Missing that deadline is contempt of court.
  • Every BUI conviction includes completing the Risk Reduction Program, and Georgia DNR says you may lose your privilege to operate a boat until you finish it.
  • Whatever the offense, it's the same program: a 130-question assessment and a 20-hour course for $360, and DDS does not accept online certificates.

Georgia's DUI school isn't only for DUI. The Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) says the program, officially the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program, is for people convicted of DUI, possession of illegal drugs, underage possession of alcohol while driving, or Boating Under the Influence (BUI).

What each of those means for your driver's license is a different story. For drug possession in particular, the rules changed in 2015, and a lot of what you'll read online is out of date. This page goes through each offense using what Georgia law and state agencies actually say, then compares all four side by side.

Does drug possession suspend your license in Georgia?

For offenses committed on or after July 1, 2015, a drug possession conviction no longer automatically suspends your Georgia driver's license. That's the result of Senate Bill 100, passed in 2015.

According to the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual, convictions for offenses on or after that date under the drug laws in Article 2 of Chapter 13 of Title 16 "will not suspend the driver's license" and shouldn't be reported to DDS, unless the offense is a felony in which a motor vehicle was used. The Georgia Attorney General reached the same conclusion in Official Opinion 2017-4: SB 100 amended O.C.G.A. § 40-5-75 to remove the mandatory suspension for drug offenses other than impaired driving, so a conviction based only on possession or other drug charges "no longer triggers an automatic, mandatory suspension."

Why DUI school can still apply after a possession charge

DDS still lists possession of illegal drugs among the convictions the program is for. Even without a license suspension, a judge may make the program part of your sentence or probation. If yours does, finish a DDS-certified program by the deadline and send your certificate to the court or your probation officer. Under DDS Rule 375-3-3-.05 the course counts only if you took it after the violation date. Read what you learn in DUI school if you're wondering what a possession charge has to do with a driving class: the course covers drug use as well as alcohol.

What if you have an older drug suspension? Form DS-566

Before SB 100, drug convictions did suspend licenses, and some of those suspensions are still sitting on driving records. DDS's Form DS-566, the Application for Reinstatement of a Controlled Substance Suspension, is the form for them. It says:

  • Time toward reinstatement eligibility starts on the date DDS processes the form onto your record, and only after any earlier suspensions on your record have been reinstated.
  • Eligibility comes after 180 days for a first controlled substance conviction in 5 years, 3 years (with early reinstatement after 1 year) for a second, and 5 years for a third, measured from arrest date to arrest date.
  • To reinstate you must submit an original certificate of completion from a DDS-approved Risk Reduction Program and a $210 reinstatement fee, or $200 if processed by mail.
  • The form is mailed to Georgia Department of Driver Services, P.O. Box 80447, Conyers, GA 30013, ATTN: CSLR Validation Unit.

If an old drug suspension is on your record, sign in to DDS Online Services or call DDS at 678-413-8400 before you file anything, so you know which rules DDS is applying to you.

DUI drugs: when drugs do suspend your license

Driving under the influence of a controlled substance or marijuana is a DUI under O.C.G.A. § 40-6-391, and the suspension rules come from O.C.G.A. § 40-5-75. The Attorney General's opinion lists DUI drugs "less safe," combined influence, and DUI drugs "per se" (any amount of a controlled substance in your blood or urine) as convictions that still suspend a license automatically. Here is what the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual lists.

And driving while suspended for DUI drugs carries a heavier fine: the DDS court manual, citing O.C.G.A. § 40-5-75(e), lists a fine of $750 to $5,000, up to 12 months in jail, or both.

Underage possession of alcohol and your license

Georgia law, O.C.G.A. § 3-3-23.1, lets a court order anyone convicted of an underage alcohol offense to complete a DDS-certified Risk Reduction Program within 120 days. Failing to finish in time is contempt of court, punishable by a fine of up to $300, up to 20 days in jail, or both. The same law says that when the charge was unlawful possession of alcohol while operating a motor vehicle, the court must report the conviction to DDS within 10 days.

Whether that conviction also suspends your license is less clear-cut, because DDS's own publications don't line up:

DDS sourceWhat it says about underage possession of alcohol while driving
ADAP teen driver manual, Chapter 1Lists "underage possession of alcohol while operating a motor vehicle" among the TADRA offenses that suspend an under-21 license for 6 months (12 months for a second)
Traffic Court Reference Manual 2021-2022Tells courts to report possession of alcohol by a minor only when the driver was operating a vehicle, and only if the violation date is before July 1, 2015; for offenses on or after that date, "do NOT report the conviction to DDS (SB100, 2015)"
Driver's Manual 2023-2024Its list of under-21 suspensions does not include underage possession of alcohol

Because of that, check your status in DDS Online Services or call DDS rather than relying on any website, this one included. If DDS does show a TADRA suspension, the court manual lists the reinstatement requirements for under-21 non-DUI suspensions as an original certificate from a DDS-approved defensive driving course, a Risk Reduction certificate if the court ordered one, and a $210 fee ($200 online or by mail). Athena offers the Driver Improvement course in Athens.

If you're under 21 and you were driving with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.02 or more, that's not a possession charge. It's a DUI, with a 6- or 12-month suspension and no limited permit. See Georgia DUI under 21.

BUI in Georgia and the Risk Reduction Program

Boating Under the Influence is covered by O.C.G.A. § 52-7-12. Georgia DNR's Law Enforcement Division says the Georgia Boat Safety Act bans operating "any boat, sailboat, personal watercraft, water skis, sailboard or similar device" while intoxicated, and also makes it illegal for an owner to let someone else operate their boat or personal watercraft while under the influence. Adults may not operate a vessel with an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more, or if drugs are detected, and people under 21 may not operate one at 0.02 or more.

The BUI statute itself doesn't address your driver's license. If you have questions about how a BUI affects anything beyond boating, ask a Georgia attorney. And note that Georgia's DBHDD rules define the Risk Reduction Program as education about alcohol and substance use "and driving a vehicle or boat," so the same certificate serves both.

How the program works, whatever your offense

Everyone takes the same program, whether the conviction was for DUI, drugs, underage alcohol or BUI.

  • Assessment: a 130-question screening (the NEEDS assessment) of your alcohol and drug use and its effect on driving. $100.
  • Intervention: a 20-hour course (Prime for Life) over several sessions, taught in a group. DDS says most people finish in less than a week. $235, plus a $25 workbook.
  • Total: $360 at every certified school, set by law. See how much DUI school costs in Georgia.
  • Online courses: DDS doesn't accept certificates from online DUI courses for any license purpose. Live virtual classes from a certified school are different. See whether you can take DUI school online in Georgia.
  • Timing: you can enroll before your case is decided, but the course must be taken after the violation date to count. See starting DUI classes before your court date.

What happens if…

  • You miss a court deadline for an underage alcohol order. The statute makes it contempt of court: up to $300, up to 20 days in jail, or both.
  • You arrive late or miss a session. DDS says late students can expect to be denied entry, and absences may mean starting over at full cost. Refunds are between you and the school under your contract.
  • You lose your certificate. The school can issue a replacement for up to $20, and DDS only requires schools to keep records for three years. If the school has closed, call DDS at 678-413-8745.
  • You need to switch schools. DDS allows an assessment transfer if your class was cancelled, you moved at least 30 miles away (transfer fee up to $25), or there's an emergency DDS approves in advance.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a possession charge suspended your license. For offenses on or after July 1, 2015, it usually didn't. Check your record before paying a reinstatement fee you may not owe.
  • Assuming a DUI drugs charge is treated like possession. It's a DUI, with a suspension and DUI school required to reinstate.
  • Taking an online course to satisfy a court order. DDS won't accept it, and courts that require a DDS-certified program won't either.
  • Waiting on a BUI. DNR ties your boating privilege to finishing the program, so the sooner you finish, the sooner you can ask about getting it back.
  • Relying on one DDS publication for an underage possession charge. They conflict; your record in DDS Online Services is what counts.

Questions to ask, and what to keep

  • Your attorney: Is my charge possession or DUI drugs? Was a vehicle used in a felony? What exactly does the sentence require and by when?
  • The clerk or probation officer: Where should the certificate go, and do you need the original?
  • DDS (678-413-8400): Does my record show a suspension at all, and if so, which reinstatement rules apply?
  • The school: Will you send my certificate to the court or probation officer, and at what cost?
  • Keep: your court order, the original certificate plus a copy, receipts, and any DDS notice, for at least three years.

Key terms

  • Risk Reduction Program: Georgia's DUI school; DBHDD rules define it as education about alcohol and substance use and driving a vehicle or boat.
  • NEEDS assessment: the 130-question screening that makes up the assessment component.
  • Certificate of Completion: the original document a certified school issues when both components are finished.
  • SB 100: the 2015 Georgia law that ended automatic license suspensions for drug convictions other than impaired driving.
  • DS-566: the DDS application for reinstating a controlled substance suspension.
  • DUI drugs: driving under the influence of a controlled substance or marijuana, including "per se" cases where any amount is found in your blood or urine.
  • BUI: operating a boat, personal watercraft or similar device under the influence, at 0.08 for adults or 0.02 under 21.
  • Habitual violator: a driver with three qualifying convictions in 5 years, whose license is revoked.

Frequently asked questions

Do you lose your license for drug possession in Georgia?

Not automatically, if the offense happened on or after July 1, 2015. A 2015 law, Senate Bill 100, ended automatic license suspensions for drug possession. A felony drug conviction can still lead to a suspension if a motor vehicle was used to commit it, and driving under the influence of drugs still suspends your license.

Is BUI the same as DUI in Georgia?

No, but they work in similar ways. BUI is operating a boat, personal watercraft or similar vessel under the influence, and DUI is driving a vehicle. Both use a 0.08 limit for adults and 0.02 for people under 21, and both require the Risk Reduction Program. The difference is that BUI penalties center on your privilege to operate a boat, while a DUI suspends your driver's license.

Do I have to take DUI school for a BUI in Georgia?

Yes. Georgia's BUI law lists completion of a DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program as part of the sentence for a first, second and third conviction, and Georgia DNR says you may lose your boating privilege until you finish it. It's the same DDS-certified program that DUI offenders take.

Can a minor be ordered to DUI school for an alcohol offense?

Yes. Georgia law lets a court order anyone convicted of an underage alcohol offense to complete a DDS-certified Risk Reduction Program within 120 days. Not finishing on time is contempt of court, punishable by a fine of up to $300, up to 20 days in jail, or both.

Is the Risk Reduction class different for drug or boating offenses?

No. Everyone takes the same DDS-certified program: a 130-question assessment and a 20-hour intervention course, for $360 total. The course covers alcohol, drug use and their effects on driving, and Georgia's rules describe the program as covering driving a vehicle or a boat.

How long is your license suspended for DUI drugs in Georgia?

DDS's court manual lists a 180-day period for a first conviction in 5 years, after which you can reinstate with a Risk Reduction certificate and a $210 fee, or $200 online or by mail. For a second conviction in 5 years the manual lists a one-year period with no limited permit; DDS's own DS-566 form describes the suspension as 3 years with early reinstatement after one year. A third conviction makes you a habitual violator.

What is Form DS-566 in Georgia?

DS-566 is the DDS Application for Reinstatement of a Controlled Substance Suspension. It applies to licenses suspended for a drug conviction, which mostly means offenses from before July 1, 2015. It says time toward reinstatement starts when DDS processes the form, and that you need an original Risk Reduction certificate and a $210 fee ($200 by mail) to reinstate.

Does a drug charge affect a commercial driver's license in Georgia?

It can. DDS and the Georgia Attorney General both say that knowingly using a motor vehicle in a felony involving making, selling, distributing or trafficking drugs, or possession with intent to do those things, brings a lifetime disqualification from driving commercial vehicles. Simple possession without a vehicle does not trigger that rule.

Can I get a limited permit after a DUI drugs conviction in Georgia?

DDS's sources don't fully agree. The DDS court manual says a limited permit may be available for a first DUI drugs conviction if the offense was committed on or after July 29, 2020, while the current Driver's Manual still lists DUI drugs among the suspensions with no permit. Check your record in DDS Online Services or call DDS at 678-413-8400.

Does a BUI show up on my Georgia driver's license?

The BUI law and the Georgia DNR page describe the penalty as losing your privilege to operate a boat or personal watercraft, and neither says a BUI suspends a driver's license. If you have questions about how a BUI affects anything beyond boating, ask a Georgia attorney.
