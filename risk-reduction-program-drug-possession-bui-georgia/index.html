Not just DUI: drug possession, underage alcohol and BUI can also require Georgia's Risk Reduction Program

Georgia DDS says DUI school is also for people convicted of drug possession, underage possession of alcohol while driving, and Boating Under the Influence. Here's when you need it, what happens to your license, and what changed in 2015.

By Athena DUI Academy & Family EnrichmentLast reviewed September 28, 20269 min read

Key takeaways

  • Georgia DDS says the Risk Reduction Program is for people convicted of DUI, possession of illegal drugs, underage possession of alcohol while driving, or Boating Under the Influence.
  • Since a 2015 law change, a drug possession offense committed on or after July 1, 2015 no longer automatically suspends your Georgia license, unless it's a felony in which a motor vehicle was used.
  • DUI drugs is different: a first conviction in 5 years suspends your license for at least 180 days, and you need a DUI school certificate to reinstate.
  • A court may order anyone convicted of an underage alcohol offense to finish DUI school within 120 days. Missing that deadline is contempt of court.
  • Every BUI conviction includes completing the Risk Reduction Program, and Georgia DNR says you may lose your privilege to operate a boat until you finish it.

Georgia's DUI school isn't only for DUI. The Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) says the program, officially the DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program, is for people convicted of DUI, possession of illegal drugs, underage possession of alcohol while driving, or Boating Under the Influence (BUI).

What each of those means for your driver's license is a different story. For drug possession in particular, the rules changed in 2015, and a lot of what you'll read online is out of date. This page goes through each offense using what Georgia law and state agencies actually say.

If a court has ordered you to take the program, your court paperwork is the final word on your deadline and what you need to turn in.

Does drug possession suspend your license in Georgia?

For offenses committed on or after July 1, 2015, a drug possession conviction no longer automatically suspends your Georgia driver's license. That's the result of Senate Bill 100, passed in 2015.

According to the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual, convictions for offenses on or after that date under the drug laws in Article 2 of Chapter 13 of Title 16 "will not suspend the driver's license" and shouldn't be reported to DDS, unless the offense is a felony in which a motor vehicle was used. The Georgia Attorney General reached the same conclusion in Official Opinion 2017-4: a conviction based only on possession or other drug charges "no longer triggers an automatic, mandatory suspension."

There are exceptions:

  • A felony involving a vehicle. If a motor vehicle was used to commit a felony, such as selling or trafficking drugs, the court reports it to DDS and a suspension can follow.
  • Commercial driving. Knowingly using a motor vehicle in a felony involving making, selling, distributing or trafficking drugs brings a lifetime disqualification from driving commercial vehicles.
  • Driving under the influence of drugs. That's a DUI, not a possession charge, and it still suspends your license. More on that below.

Why DUI school can still apply after a possession charge

DDS still lists possession of illegal drugs among the convictions the program is for. Even without a license suspension, a judge may make the program part of your sentence or probation. If yours does, finish a DDS-certified program by the deadline and send your certificate to the court or your probation officer.

What if you have an older drug suspension?

Before SB 100, drug convictions did suspend licenses. Georgia law now authorizes DDS to reinstate, immediately, a license that was suspended for a drug offense (other than DUI drugs) that happened before July 1, 2015, if that license hasn't already been reinstated. DDS still posts Form DS-566, the Application for Reinstatement of a Controlled Substance Suspension, which asks for a Risk Reduction certificate and a $210 fee ($200 by mail). If an old drug suspension is still on your record, call DDS at 678-413-8400 to find out which rules apply to you.

DUI drugs: when drugs do suspend your license

Driving under the influence of a controlled substance or marijuana is treated differently from possession. Under O.C.G.A. § 40-5-75:

  • First conviction in 5 years: your license is suspended for at least 180 days. After that, you can apply to reinstate with proof that you completed the Risk Reduction Program and a $210 fee, or $200 by mail.
  • Second conviction in 5 years: a 3-year suspension, but you can apply for reinstatement after one year with proof of the Risk Reduction Program and the fee.
  • Third conviction in 5 years: you're declared a habitual violator and your license is revoked.

DUI drugs is still a DUI. If you have two or more DUI convictions of any kind within 10 years, you'll also need a clinical evaluation, and any treatment it recommends, before reinstatement. See Second DUI in Georgia: the clinical evaluation.

Underage possession of alcohol and your license

Georgia law, O.C.G.A. § 3-3-23.1, lets a court order anyone convicted of an underage alcohol offense to complete a DDS-certified Risk Reduction Program within 120 days. Failing to finish in time is contempt of court, punishable by a fine of up to $300, up to 20 days in jail, or both. The same law says that when the charge was unlawful possession of alcohol while operating a motor vehicle, the court must report the conviction to DDS within 10 days.

Whether that conviction also suspends your license is less clear-cut. DDS's teen driver (ADAP) manual lists underage possession of alcohol while driving as an offense that suspends a license for 6 months, or 12 months for a second. But the current DDS Driver's Manual doesn't include it in its list of under-21 suspensions, and the DDS Traffic Court Reference Manual says under-21 alcohol violations on or after July 1, 2015 shouldn't be reported to DDS. Because DDS's own publications don't line up, check your status in DDS Online Services or call DDS rather than relying on any website, this one included.

If you're under 21 and you were driving with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.02 or more, that's not a possession charge. It's a DUI, with a 6- or 12-month suspension and no limited permit. See Georgia DUI under 21.

BUI in Georgia and the Risk Reduction Program

Boating Under the Influence is covered by O.C.G.A. § 52-7-12. Georgia DNR says the ban covers boats, sailboats, personal watercraft, water skis, sailboards and similar devices. Adults may not operate a vessel with an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more, and people under 21 may not operate one at 0.02 or more.

The BUI law lists completion of a DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program as part of the sentence for a first, second and third conviction. It also lists a clinical evaluation and, if recommended, a substance abuse treatment program. The Georgia Department of Natural Resources (DNR) says a person arrested for BUI may lose the privilege to operate a boat or personal watercraft until they complete a DDS-approved Risk Reduction Program, and that refusing a test can cost you that privilege for up to one year.

How the program works, whatever your offense

Everyone takes the same program, whether the conviction was for DUI, drugs, underage alcohol or BUI. Georgia's rules describe it as education about alcohol and substance use and driving a vehicle or a boat.

  • Assessment: a 130-question screening of your alcohol and drug use and its effect on driving.
  • Intervention: a 20-hour course over several sessions, taught in a group. DDS says most people finish in less than a week.
  • Cost: $360 at every certified school ($100 assessment, $235 course, $25 workbook). See how much DUI school costs in Georgia.
  • Online courses: DDS doesn't accept certificates from online DUI courses. Live virtual classes from a certified school are different. See whether you can take DUI school online in Georgia.

If your license is suspended, sign in to DDS Online Services to see exactly what you need to reinstate. For a step-by-step overview of the program, read our complete guide to Georgia DUI school.

Frequently asked questions

Do you lose your license for drug possession in Georgia?

Not automatically, if the offense happened on or after July 1, 2015. A 2015 law, Senate Bill 100, ended automatic license suspensions for drug possession. A felony drug conviction can still lead to a suspension if a motor vehicle was used to commit it, and driving under the influence of drugs still suspends your license.

Is BUI the same as DUI in Georgia?

No, but they work in similar ways. BUI is operating a boat, personal watercraft or similar vessel under the influence, and DUI is driving a vehicle. Both use a 0.08 limit for adults and 0.02 for people under 21, and both require the Risk Reduction Program. The difference is that BUI penalties center on your privilege to operate a boat, while a DUI suspends your driver's license.

Do I have to take DUI school for a BUI in Georgia?

Yes. Georgia's BUI law lists completion of a DUI Alcohol or Drug Use Risk Reduction Program as part of the sentence for a first, second and third conviction. It's the same DDS-certified program that DUI offenders take.

Can a minor be ordered to DUI school for an alcohol offense?

Yes. Georgia law lets a court order anyone convicted of an underage alcohol offense to complete a DDS-certified Risk Reduction Program within 120 days. Not finishing on time is contempt of court, punishable by a fine of up to $300, up to 20 days in jail, or both.

Is the Risk Reduction class different for drug or boating offenses?

No. Everyone takes the same DDS-certified program: a 130-question assessment and a 20-hour intervention course, for $360 total. The course covers alcohol, drug use and their effects on driving, and Georgia's rules describe the program as covering driving a vehicle or a boat.

How long is your license suspended for DUI drugs in Georgia?

For a first conviction with no other in 5 years, at least 180 days. After that you can apply to reinstate with a Risk Reduction certificate and a $210 fee, or $200 by mail. A second conviction within 5 years means a 3-year suspension, with the option to apply for reinstatement after one year.
