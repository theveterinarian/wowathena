Who takes anger management classes?

We serve Athens-Clarke County and the surrounding Northeast Georgia area. People take anger management for one of these reasons:

  • A court order. A judge made the class part of your sentence or another court requirement.
  • Probation. Georgia's Department of Community Supervision says courts may impose special conditions depending on the offense, and standard conditions let your officer direct you to rehabilitative services.
  • Personal goals. You, your family or your employer want you to learn better ways to deal with anger.

How to read your court or probation order

The number of hours comes from your court or probation order, not from the school. We can't choose the hours for you, and neither can any other school. Before you enroll, find these five things in your paperwork.

  1. The program name. Does it say "anger management," "Family Violence Intervention Program," "FVIP," "batterer's intervention," or something like "alcohol and drug treatment"? Each is a different program.
  2. The amount. A number of hours, a number of sessions or weeks, or just "complete a program."
  3. The deadline. A date to finish, or a date to file proof, or "within 90 days of sentencing" style language.
  4. Who gets the proof. The court clerk, your probation officer or both.
  5. Any format limits. Some orders say "in person" or require a specific provider or a "certified" program.

Anger management vs. FVIP: the difference, with the rule

If your order says "Family Violence Intervention Program" or "FVIP," anger management will not satisfy it. The Georgia Commission on Family Violence says Georgia's FVIP standards set these programs apart from anger management. Athena does not offer FVIP. Find a certified program in the Commission's directory by county; you may attend any certified FVIP in the state.

According to the Commission, anger management sees anger as the trigger for a person's reaction. FVIPs are built specifically for people who have harmed an intimate partner or family member, and they treat abuse as learned behavior aimed at control. Under O.C.G.A. § 19-13-16(a), a court sentencing someone for an offense involving family violence, or issuing a family violence protective order, must order an FVIP unless it states on the record why one isn't appropriate.

Georgia's FVIP rules, in Department of Community Supervision Rule 105-3-.07, show how different the two programs are:

What an anger management class typically covers

Anger management is about practical skills, not blame. The clearest public description of what these classes teach is the participant workbook published by the U.S. Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA), Anger Management for Substance Use Disorder and Mental Health Clients. It's a 12-session, cognitive-behavioral program in the public domain, and its outline is a good guide to what to expect from a course like this. Ask us what your specific class covers when you call.

Session (SAMHSA workbook)What you work on
1. OverviewAnger vs. aggression vs. hostility, myths about anger, and the "anger meter" for rating anger from 1 to 10.
2. Events and cuesThe events and "red flags" that set you off, and the four kinds of cues: physical, behavioral, emotional and cognitive.
3. Anger control plansImmediate strategies like timeouts, deep breathing and thought stopping, plus preventive ones like exercise.
4. The aggression cycleBuildup, explosion and aftermath, and progressive muscle relaxation.
5. Cognitive restructuringThe A-B-C-D model: activating event, beliefs, consequences and disputing the beliefs that fuel anger.
6 and 11. Practice sessionsWorking through real situations with the tools so far.
7 and 8. Assertiveness and conflict resolutionThe difference between aggressive, passive, passive-aggressive and assertive responses, and a step-by-step conflict resolution model.
9 and 10. Anger and the familyHow anger was expressed in the family you grew up in and how that shapes you now.
12. Closing and graduationReviewing your anger control plan and receiving a certificate.

The SAMHSA workbook also spells out group rules that most programs share in some form: no violence or threats, confidentiality, short between-session assignments, and an attendance limit. In that model, missing more than three of the 12 sessions means no certificate. For a closer look at the material, read what you learn in anger management classes.

Proof of completion

The certificate is the whole point for a court or probation case, so handle it carefully.

  • Ask what it must show. Most courts and officers want your name, the program name, the number of hours or sessions and the completion date. Ask your probation officer whether anything else is required, such as attendance records.
  • Ask where it goes. The clerk, your officer or both. When needed, Athena sends the certificate to the court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost. Tell us who should receive it when you register.
  • Keep your own copy. Keep the certificate, your registration confirmation and any receipts until your case and probation are fully closed. If a record is ever questioned, your copy answers it.
  • File before the deadline. A certificate that arrives late may not count, and only the court or your officer can extend the date.

What happens if…

Your order says FVIP and you took anger management

The class won't satisfy the order. Georgia treats FVIP as a separate certified program, and FVIPs send compliance and termination notices to courts and probation. Enroll in a certified FVIP from the Commission's directory and tell your officer right away.

You miss the deadline

Only the court or your probation officer can change the terms of your order. Talk to them or your attorney early, and keep proof that you registered. Because you can't register and start class the same day, give yourself room.

You want to take it online

Athena's live online class is taught in real time with an instructor, not as a self-paced video. Whether that meets your requirement depends on your court or officer, so confirm before you register. See Can you take anger management classes online in Georgia? for the questions to ask.

You miss a class

Each program sets its own attendance rules. Ask about make-ups before you enroll, and call us before the session if something comes up.

Questions to ask your probation officer

  • Is my requirement anger management or FVIP, and how many hours or sessions?
  • Is there a date to finish, and a separate date to file proof?
  • Will you accept a live online class, or does it have to be in person?
  • Do you need a specific provider, or any provider that issues a certificate?
  • What must the certificate show, and should the program send it to you or to the clerk?
  • Does this class also satisfy any other condition on my sentence, such as counseling?

Key terms

  • Anger management: a skills class that treats anger as the trigger for a reaction and teaches ways to control it.
  • FVIP: a Family Violence Intervention Program, a 24-class program certified by the Department of Community Supervision for people who have abused an intimate partner or family member.
  • Special condition of probation: a requirement a court adds to a sentence based on the offense, such as treatment or a class.
  • Certificate of completion: the program's proof that you finished; the document your court or officer needs.
  • Anger vs. aggression: in the SAMHSA model, anger is a feeling; aggression is behavior meant to cause harm.
  • Timeout: a basic anger control strategy of leaving a situation before it escalates.
  • Protective order: a court order against family violence; issuing one requires the court to order FVIP unless it states why not.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours of anger management do I need in Georgia?

Your court or probation order sets the number of hours, so the answer is on your paperwork. Georgia's rules fix a length for FVIP, 24 weekly classes, but for anger management the length comes from your order. If the order isn't clear, ask your probation officer, the court clerk or your attorney before you enroll, then call us so we can match you with the right class.

Is anger management the same as a Family Violence Intervention Program?

No. The Georgia Commission on Family Violence says Georgia's FVIP standards set FVIPs apart from anger management. Under Rule 105-3-.07, an FVIP is 24 weekly group classes of at least 90 minutes, certified by the Department of Community Supervision. If your order says FVIP, you need a certified FVIP, and Athena does not offer one.

What does a court-ordered anger management class cover?

Programs vary, but SAMHSA's public anger management workbook shows the usual ground: telling anger from aggression, spotting the events and cues that set you off, building an anger control plan with timeouts and relaxation, understanding the buildup-explosion-aftermath cycle, changing the self-talk behind anger, and practicing assertiveness and conflict resolution. Call us to ask what your class covers.

Can I take court ordered anger management online in Georgia?

Athena teaches anger management live online or in person in Athens. Whether a live online class meets your requirement depends on your court or probation officer, so check with them first. When in doubt, the in-person class at our office is an option.

What proof of completion does the court need?

Usually a certificate from the program showing your name, the program name and the hours or sessions completed, sent to the court or probation officer by the deadline in your order. Ask your probation officer exactly what the certificate must show and where it should go. Athena sends certificates to the court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost.

What happens if I miss the deadline in my order?

Only the court or your probation officer can change the terms of your order, so talk to them or your attorney as early as possible. Because you can't register and start class the same day, enroll as soon as you know the requirement. Keep your registration confirmation as proof that you started.

Can I take anger management if I'm not court ordered?

Yes. Athena's anger management classes are open to people taking them for personal goals, not only for court or probation. Call 706.215.9661 to find a class time that fits your week.

How much does an FVIP cost compared with anger management?

Georgia caps FVIP fees at $60 per class and $120 for intake, and the Commission on Family Violence says classes average $25 to $30, with a sliding scale for people the court declares indigent. Athena's anger management pricing isn't published, so call for a quote. The two are not interchangeable, so the price only matters once you know which one your order requires.

Does probation require anger management in Georgia?

It can. The Department of Community Supervision says courts may impose special conditions depending on the offense, and standard conditions let your officer direct you to rehabilitative services. Your sentence and your probation paperwork say what applies to you.

Will Athena send proof of completion to my probation officer?

Yes, when it's needed. Athena sends completion certificates to your court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost. Tell us who should receive it when you register.
