Who takes anger management classes?

We serve Athens-Clarke County and the surrounding Northeast Georgia area. People take anger management for one of these reasons:

  • A court order. A judge made the class part of your sentence or another court requirement.
  • Probation. A probation officer requires the class as part of your supervision.
  • Personal goals. You, your family or your employer want you to learn better ways to deal with anger.

If you're taking the class for court or probation, the next section matters most.

How many hours of anger management do you need?

The number of hours comes from your court or probation order, not from the school. We can't choose the hours for you, and neither can any other school. Some orders also say what type of program you must attend.

Before you enroll, look for three things in your paperwork:

  1. The number of hours or sessions required.
  2. The name of the program. Does it say "anger management," or something else, such as "Family Violence Intervention Program"?
  3. Any deadline for finishing the class or turning in proof.

Then call us. We'll help you find the class that lines up with your order. If the wording is unclear, check with your probation officer, the court clerk or your attorney first. Our article How long are court-ordered anger management classes? goes into more detail.

If your order says "Family Violence Intervention Program" or "FVIP," anger management will not satisfy it. The Georgia Commission on Family Violence says Georgia's FVIP standards set these programs apart from anger management. FVIPs are 24-week state-certified programs designed for family violence offenders. You need to enroll in a certified FVIP, which you can find in the Commission's directory of certified programs by county. Athena does not offer FVIP.

Anger management vs. FVIP: what's the difference?

The two can sound alike, but Georgia treats them as different programs. According to the Commission on Family Violence, anger management sees anger as the trigger for a person's reaction. FVIPs are built specifically for people who have harmed an intimate partner or family member, and they treat abuse as learned behavior aimed at control.

Under Georgia law, a court sentencing someone for an offense involving family violence, or issuing a family violence protective order, must order an FVIP unless the court states on the record why it isn't appropriate. So if your case involves family violence, check your order carefully before you sign up for any class.

What you work on in class

Anger management is about practical skills, not blame. Courses generally help you notice what sets you off, slow down before you react, and talk through conflict without it getting worse. The aim is skills you can use in daily life.

For a closer look at typical topics, read what you learn in anger management classes.

Live online or in person

You can take Athena's anger management classes live online from home or in person at our Athens office. Live online classes are taught in real time with an instructor, not as a self-paced video.

If you're taking the class for court or probation, confirm that a live online class meets your requirement before you register. Your probation officer or the court can tell you. See Can you take anger management classes online in Georgia? for the questions to ask.

How to register

  1. Read your order. Note the hours, the program name and any deadline.
  2. Call us at 706.215.9661, Monday to Friday, 10am to 6pm, or register online. We'll confirm the right class and give you current pricing.
  3. Join the next scheduled class. Classes don't start the same day you register, so sign up early if you have a deadline.
  4. Finish the class and get your certificate. When needed, we send your certificate to the court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost.

If you're up against a deadline

Court and probation orders often come with a date to finish the class or turn in proof. Because you can't register and start class the same day, give yourself room. Sign up as soon as you know what your order requires.

If something outside your control means you might miss the deadline, talk to your probation officer or attorney early. Only the court or your probation officer can change the terms of your order. Keep a copy of your registration and your certificate for your own records.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours of anger management do I need in Georgia?

Your court or probation order sets the number of hours, so the answer is on your paperwork. If the order isn't clear, ask your probation officer, the court clerk or your attorney before you enroll, then call us so we can match you with the right class.

Is anger management the same as a Family Violence Intervention Program?

No. The Georgia Commission on Family Violence says Georgia's FVIP standards set FVIPs apart from anger management. FVIPs are 24-week certified programs for family violence offenders. If your order says FVIP, you need a certified FVIP, and Athena does not offer one.

Can I take court ordered anger management online in Georgia?

Athena teaches anger management live online or in person in Athens. Whether a live online class meets your requirement depends on your court or probation officer, so check with them first. When in doubt, the in-person class at our office is an option.

Can I take anger management if I'm not court ordered?

Yes. Athena's anger management classes are open to people taking them for personal goals, not only for court or probation. Call 706.215.9661 to find a class time that fits your week.

Will Athena send proof of completion to my probation officer?

Yes, when it's needed. Athena sends completion certificates to your court, probation officer or clerk at no extra cost. Tell us who should receive it when you register.
